\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Attention encodes no order by itself, and the mechanisms proposed to supply it now
number in the dozens. This review argues the space is far smaller than the list.

If the positional map is linear in the query and key, translation-invariant and
continuous, the interval operators form a one-parameter matrix group
$A(d)=\exp(dM)$, so classifying positional encodings reduces to classifying the
generator $M$ up to conjugacy --- which leaves exactly two live slots, a real part
that scales and an imaginary part that rotates, because $\C$ has exactly two.
Defective generators add polynomial factors and nothing else. Writing both slots as
one complex log-polar accumulator makes every mechanism a choice of where content
enters: magnitude or phase, instantaneous or accumulated, at what sign and what rank.
Mechanisms that appear to escape --- PaTH, DeltaNet, RWKV-7, CoPE, DAPE, MesaNet,
Titans, TAPE, TEM-t, and the classical relative methods of Shaw, Transformer-XL and
T5 --- escape by breaking one of the three assumptions, and it is possible to say
which.

Two consequences do not follow from any single mechanism. The two slots answer
\emph{different questions}: a signed accumulated phase measures net displacement and
is a map, a monotone one measures elapsed path and is a clock, and the two are
mutually exclusive --- so the monotone increments of CARoPE, GRAPE-AP and CoPE are
correct on language and disqualifying on navigation. That the distinction is invisible
on text is not incidental: in one content-dependent mechanism's own ablation, NoPE
attains the lowest perplexity of every encoding tested. And the frame has a second
reading, in which the positional operator is an associative memory's transport
kernel; under it the fast-weight literature and the positional-encoding literature
are seen to have divided the two slots between them without either saying so.

The review closes with what the frame implies for choosing a mechanism for a given
task. The measurements that test its two central claims --- the sign and the
input-side rank of the content-to-phase map, and the map/clock crossover --- are
reported in a companion paper, \emph{What the Axes Are Worth}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Attention computes content-dependent interactions between tokens and, by itself,
encodes no order at all: permute the inputs and the outputs permute with them. A
positional mechanism is whatever is added to restore order, and the space of such
mechanisms is now large --- absolute embeddings, relative biases, rotary phases,
decays, gates, content-dependent variants of each, and their state-space analogues.

This review argues that the space is much smaller than the list suggests.

\paragraph{The thesis.} If the positional map is linear in the query and key,
translation-invariant, and continuous, then the family of interval operators is a
one-parameter matrix group $A(d) = \exp(dM)$, and classifying positional encodings
reduces to classifying the generator $M$ up to conjugacy. Diagonalising $M$ leaves
exactly two live slots --- a real part that scales and an imaginary part that
rotates --- because $\C$ has exactly two. Writing both as one complex log-polar
accumulator turns every mechanism in the arena into a choice of \emph{where content
enters}: in the magnitude or the phase, instantaneously or accumulated, with what
sign and at what rank. Mechanisms that appear to escape this picture escape by
breaking one of the three assumptions, and it is usually possible to say which.

\paragraph{What the frame buys.} Two things a list of mechanisms does not.
First, the two slots turn out to answer \emph{different questions} rather than to be
two settings of one dial: a signed accumulated phase measures net displacement and is
a map, while a monotone one measures elapsed path and is a clock, and the two are
mutually exclusive by construction ($\S$\ref{sec:clockmap}). Second, the frame makes
visible two axes on which the mechanisms genuinely differ and which nobody varies
deliberately --- the \emph{sign} of the increment and the \emph{input-side rank} of
the content-to-phase map --- and both are invisible on the tasks the literature
evaluates.

\paragraph{Scope, and what this review is not.} It covers positional mechanisms as
operators on the attention logit: how position enters, what algebra it lives in, and
what that implies for what can be computed. It does not cover the
frequency-allocation and context-extension line (Position Interpolation, NTK-aware
scaling, YaRN, LongRoPE), which varies the readout rather than the operator and is
surveyed elsewhere; $\S$\ref{sec:readout} states only what that line contributes to
the frame. Original measurements are reported in the companion paper and are
marked as such throughout.

\paragraph{Positioning.} Four surveys of this material were checked. Three --- Li
(arXiv:2608.10021, August 2026) on absolute and relative methods, RoPE and
long-context scaling; the length-extrapolation survey (arXiv:2312.17044); and the
infinite-extrapolation framework of Vetcha (arXiv:2601.06113) --- contain no
content-dependent \emph{phase} mechanism at all: between them, zero occurrences of
MapFormer, Selective RoPE, CARoPE, FoX, GRAPE or Mamba-3, and CoPE and Mamba twice
each. The fourth, Zhu et al.'s long-context survey (arXiv:2503.17407, March
2025), \emph{does} carry a category for it, ``content-aware position embedding'',
with two members: CoPE and DAPE. That category is the closest existing treatment of
this material, and it predates almost all of it --- CARoPE by four months, Selective
RoPE, MapFormer and GRAPE by eight to nine, Mamba-3 by a year.

So the gap is not that nobody noticed content-dependence. It is that the cell was
opened on the \emph{additive} side, by DAPE (2024) and CoPE (2024), and everything
that has since filled the \emph{multiplicative} side arrived after the last survey
that had a place to put it. The memory reading of $\S$\ref{sec:memory} and the
navigation regime appear in none of the five.

\paragraph{The question this is in service of.} The interest here is not
positional encoding for its own sake. It is positional encoding as the mechanism by
which a model learns a \emph{relational} representation --- a ``where'' --- and the
claim under test is the one TEM makes and MapFormer inherits: that learning the
where, and keeping it \emph{separated} from the ``what'', is what allows transfer to
new environments and to new problems sharing the same relational structure. A
sensory code is environment-specific and must be rebuilt every time; a structural
code is not, and can be carried over. On that reading the axes below are not
descriptive categories but the design space of the where, and
the companion paper states what the separation is measured to buy.

\paragraph{What is claimed, and what is not.} Three claims, in descending order
of how much of the evidence is ours.

\textbf{C1 (analytical).} The arena is a classification rather than a list: under
linearity, translation invariance and continuity the interval operators form a
one-parameter group, leaving two live slots, and every mechanism that appears to
escape breaks one of the three assumptions in a way that can be named. \emph{The
classification itself is not ours} --- Puranik states it, GRAPE and Mamba-3
independently state the two-slot decomposition. The contribution is the audit: the
placement of every mechanism in $\S$\ref{sec:map}, the escape routes in
$\S$\ref{sec:escape}, and the observation that the fast-weight literature has been
occupying the same two slots from the other side ($\S$\ref{sec:memory}).

\textbf{C2 (conceptual, and tested).} The two slots answer \emph{different
questions}. A signed accumulated phase measures net displacement and is a map; a
monotone one measures elapsed path and is a clock; they are mutually exclusive by
construction. The consequence is that a monotone increment is not a defect --- it is
correct on language and disqualifying on navigation. This is falsifiable and is
tested by crossover in the companion paper: on a task built to want a clock the
cost of forcing monotonicity falls from $0.28$ to nothing measurable, and an
architecture free to choose adopts a different accumulator per task. Had the cost
been the same on both, the claim would be withdrawn.

\textbf{C3 (empirical).} Two properties of the content-to-phase map that no paper
in this literature varies deliberately --- the \emph{sign} of the increment and its
\emph{input-side rank} --- are worth more per parameter than anything else measured
here, and both dominate the choice of positional \emph{encoding}.

\emph{Not claimed}: the taxonomy and the closure argument (C1's caveat above); the
content-dependent rotation, which MapFormer, Selective RoPE and Mamba-3 arrived at
independently; that a signed increment is generally better, which C2 denies; and
that a fixed index code cannot perform contextual counting, which is CoPE's result
and is reproduced here in another architecture as an anchor
(the companion paper).

\paragraph{Organisation.} $\S$\ref{sec:frame}--\ref{sec:classification} set up the
polar frame and the classification. $\S$\ref{sec:classical} places the classical
relative methods, which fail the classification's third condition.
$\S$\ref{sec:phase}--\ref{sec:pope} treat the two slots in turn and
$\S$\ref{sec:unify} unifies them in one equation. $\S$\ref{sec:clockmap} is the
conceptual consequence. $\S$\ref{sec:map} is the relational map over eight axes;
$\S$\ref{sec:emtem} places two mechanisms from the cognitive-map literature and
$\S$\ref{sec:memory} re-reads the whole frame from the fast-weight side.
$\S$\ref{sec:readout} covers what the long-context line contributes,
the companion paper reports the measurements, and $\S$\ref{sec:capability} draws
out what they imply for choosing a mechanism.


\section{The shared frame}
\label{sec:frame}

Rotary attention transforms the query and key of head $h$ by a block-diagonal
rotation before the inner product. Splitting a head's $\hd$ coordinates into
$\nb = \hd/2$ planes and identifying each plane with $\C$, write
$\tilde q_t^{(c)}, \tilde k_s^{(c)} \in \C$ for the query at position $t$ and key at
position $s$ in plane $c$. The pre-softmax logit is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:polar}
  a_{ts} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_{c=1}^{\nb}
    \mu^q_{t,c}\,\mu^k_{s,c}\,
    \cos\!\big(\theta^{(c)}_s - \theta^{(c)}_t + \psi^k_{s,c} - \psi^q_{t,c}\big),
\end{equation}
in polar coordinates $\tilde q = \mu^q e^{i(\psi^q + \theta_t)}$,
$\tilde k = \mu^k e^{i(\psi^k + \theta_s)}$. Every logit is a sum of products
$\mu\cos\varphi$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{A positional mechanism is a choice about one of those two
factors.} RoPE, MapFormer, Selective RoPE, CARoPE and Mamba-3's rotation set the
phase $\varphi$. PoPE, ALiBi, the Forgetting Transformer and every gated linear RNN
set the magnitude $\mu$. xPos, MapPoPE and Mamba-3 set both. That is the whole
design space of \eqref{eq:polar}, and $\S$\ref{sec:classification} explains why it
is not an artefact of the parameterisation.


\section{The classification, and why there are two slots}
\label{sec:classification}

Let $\tilde q_t = F(t)q_t$ and $\tilde k_s = G(s)k_s$, so the logit is
$q_t^\top F(t)^\top G(s) k_s$, and impose three conditions:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Linearity.} $F$ and $G$ act linearly on $q$ and $k$.
\item \textbf{Translation invariance.} $F(t)^\top G(s)$ depends only on $s-t$.
\item \textbf{Continuity} in the interval.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Normalise so $F(t)^\top G(t) = I$, absorbing the constant into the keys.
Then $A(d) := F(0)^\top G(d)$ satisfies $A(0) = I$ and $A(d)A(e) = A(d+e)$: the
interval operators form a \emph{one-parameter matrix group}, so $A(d) = \exp(dM)$
for a fixed generator $M$, and the classification of positional encodings is the
classification of $M$ up to conjugacy. This argument is due to
Puranik (2026); GRAPE (Zhang et al., ICLR 2026) develops the same framework
independently and uses it to prove exact special cases. The two-slot decomposition
itself has now been stated at least three times independently: as multiplicative
rotations plus additive logit biases (GRAPE, December 2025), as ``a multiplicative
transformation and an additive bias'' indexed by the relative position
(Vetcha, January 2026), and as the real and imaginary parts of one generator
(Puranik, April 2026). It should be treated as settled rather than as anyone's
contribution.

Putting $M$ in Jordan form gives three cases and no others.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Diagonalisable, real eigenvalue $\lambda$.} The plane contributes
  $e^{\lambda d}$: exponential decay for $\lambda<0$, NoPE at $\lambda=0$, and a
  blow-up for $\lambda>0$ that no causal design wants.
\item \textbf{Diagonalisable, conjugate pair.} The real canonical form is
  $e^{\lambda d}\,\mathrm{rot}(\omega d)$ --- \emph{damped RoPE}. This is the
  $\operatorname{Re}/\operatorname{Im}$ split of \eqref{eq:polar}, and
  \textbf{there are exactly two slots because $\C$ has exactly two}. RetNet, xPos
  and Mamba-3 all live here.
\item \textbf{Defective.} Repeated eigenvalues with a nilpotent part give
  polynomial factors $d^r$ times the above. ALiBi is realisable this way after
  augmenting $q$ and $k$; Jordan-RoPE (2026) is the deliberate occupant, coupling a
  complex eigenvalue to a nilpotent in the same block to obtain modes
  $d^r e^{i\omega d}$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent A remark of Puranik's is worth isolating because it is the bridge to
everything content-dependent: a gate with a data-dependent decay should be read as
\emph{learning how far to advance time}, not as changing a rate. That is exactly
the substitution $t \mapsto S_t = \sum_{u\le t}G(x_u)$ of $\S$\ref{sec:unify}.


\section{The classical relative methods}
\label{sec:classical}

Shaw-style relative position representations, Transformer-XL and T5's relative bias
predate the rotary line and are still widely deployed. They are one of the four escape routes of
$\S$\ref{sec:map}, and the one whose route out is least obvious.

Shaw et al.\ add a learned vector indexed by the \emph{clipped} lag,
\[
  s_{ij} = \frac{q_i^{\!\top}\big(k_j + a^K_{r_{ij}}\big)}{\sqrt{\hd}},
  \qquad r_{ij} = \mathrm{clip}(j-i,\,-K,\,K),
\]
and T5 compresses the same idea to a per-head scalar,
$s_{ij} \mathrel{+}= b_{h,\,\mathrm{bucket}(j-i)}$. Transformer-XL carries the
relative form across cached segments.

They are linear in $q$ and $k$ and translation-invariant, and they fail only on
continuity --- $\mathrm{clip}$ and $\mathrm{bucket}$ are step functions, so no
generator exists and beyond the table's range the operator is constant rather than
continuing.

The trade-off is exact and worth stating, because it is the one design choice the
rotary line silently makes. \textbf{A table can represent an arbitrary function of
the lag inside its window and cannot extrapolate outside it at all; a one-parameter
group is restricted to the three families of $\S$\ref{sec:classification} and
extrapolates by construction.} RoPE gives up expressiveness over short lags to buy
a functional form that is defined at every lag. Whether that purchase is honoured is
a separate question, and the long-context literature's own conclusion is that it
frequently is not: the ability to \emph{compute} positional features beyond the
training length does not imply reliable generalisation there, which is why context
extension is evaluated by short-context retention, position-wise perplexity,
retrieval and long-context reasoning rather than by whether the encoding is defined.



\section{The phase axis: a family of scans}
\label{sec:phase}

Every content-dependent phase mechanism in the arena maintains a state by
cumulative summation and reads it out linearly:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gen}
  s_t \;=\; s_{t-1} + \varphi\big(x_{t-K+1},\dots,x_t\big) \in \R^m,
  \qquad
  \theta_t \;=\; A\,s_t \in \R^{H\nb},
\end{equation}
and they differ in three numbers: the \textbf{state rank} $m$, the form of the
increment $\varphi$, and its \textbf{temporal support} $K$.

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\small
\caption{The generator family. Every content-dependent phase mechanism maintains a state by cumulative summation and reads it out linearly; they differ in three numbers.}
\label{tab:gen}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
mechanism & $m$ & $\varphi$ & $K$ & readout $A$ \\
\midrule
RoPE           & $1$    & $\equiv 1$ (constant)                   & $1$ & $\omega$ \\
MapFormer      & $r$    & $W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}x$      & $1$ & $\operatorname{diag}(\omega)$ \\
CARoPE         & $H$    & $1/(\softplus(xW)+1) \in (0,1)$         & $1$ & geometric \\
Selective RoPE & $H\nb$ & $\sigma(W_gx)\odot(\kappa * W_\omega x)$ & $4$ & $\tau I$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent RoPE is the $\varphi \equiv 1$ special case: $\theta_t = t\,\omega$ is a
clock that advances by the same amount on every token. Everything else lets the
content set the rate.

\subsection{Nesting}
\label{sec:incl}

RoPE $\subset$ MapFormer $\subset$ Selective RoPE, each inclusion \emph{removing a
constraint} rather than adding a mechanism.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{MapFormer inside Selective RoPE.} Four settings suffice: (i) the
convolution kernel becomes a delta at the last tap, so $K$ collapses to $1$; (ii)
the gate bias is set to a constant, making $\sigma(\cdot)$ a scalar $g$; (iii) the
angle projection is set to
$A/(\tau g)$ where $A = \operatorname{diag}(\omega)W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}$;
(iv) the projection bias is zeroed. The two models then compute the same $\theta$ to
$1.5\times10^{-5}$ in float32, and the constructed $W_\omega$ has rank $2$ of $64$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{RoPE inside Selective RoPE} is unconditional: $W_\omega := 0$ with
bias $b$ gives $\theta_t = \tau(t{+}1)b$, an exact index clock (constant to
$7\times10^{-9}$).

\medskip
\noindent\emph{RoPE inside MapFormer} costs something, because $\varphi$ has no bias
term: a constant increment requires every embedding row to share its projection onto
$W_{\mathrm{in}}$. Taking $W_{\mathrm{in}} = [I_r\;0]$ and pinning the first $r$
embedding coordinates achieves it exactly, at a price of $r$ of the $d$ embedding
dimensions.

\medskip
\noindent Consequently \textbf{every comparison inside this column asks whether a
constraint is worth keeping, and none of them is about expressivity.}

\subsection{The factorisation identity}

Cumulative summation is linear, so it commutes with the readout:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:factor}
  \theta_t \;=\; \omega \odot \textstyle\sum_{u \le t} W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}x_u
  \;=\; \operatorname{diag}(\omega)\,W_{\mathrm{out}}
        \textstyle\sum_{u\le t} W_{\mathrm{in}}x_u .
\end{equation}
All $H\nb = 64$ of MapFormer's phase channels are linear readouts of a single
$r$-dimensional accumulator (checked against the implementation at
$1.5\times10^{-5}$ in float32). The multi-scale frequency ladder is a \emph{view} of
one quantity, not $64$ independent clocks --- which is what makes the phase a
coherent position code rather than a bag of unrelated oscillators.

\subsection{Coherence rank}
\label{sec:rank}

Define the \textbf{coherence rank} $\rho$ as the dimension of the affine span of
$\{\theta_t\}$, i.e.\ $\operatorname{rank}[\,A\!\cdot\!B \mid A\varphi(0)\,]$ when
$\varphi(x) = Bx$. The affine part matters: RoPE's constant $\varphi$ has $B=0$, so
the linear rank alone would read $0$. Then $\rho = 1$ for RoPE, $r$ for MapFormer,
and generically $H\nb$ for Selective RoPE.

Two thresholds bracket the useful range. Below, a packing argument: if $N^D$
positions are carried by a $\rho$-dimensional code, the minimum separation of
distinct positions falls at worst like
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:packing}
  \min_{p\neq p'} \lVert \theta_p - \theta_{p'}\rVert \;\lesssim\; N^{-\max(D/\rho,\,1)},
\end{equation}
saturating at the lattice's own spacing once $\rho \ge D$. This is an upper bound
and is not tight: measured exponents track it at $D\le3$ and run steeper at $D=5$
($-2.99$ against a predicted $-2.50$). Above, once $\rho \ge D$ the bound says
nothing further, and what remains is a question about conditioning rather than
capacity --- see the companion paper.

\subsection{What $K>1$ costs}
\label{sec:group}

With $K=1$ and $\varphi$ linear, \eqref{eq:gen} is exactly the action of the free
\emph{commutative} monoid on tokens: the phase depends on the multiset of tokens
traversed and nothing else, so
$\theta(a\ \text{then}\ b) = \theta(b\ \text{then}\ a)$ and
$\prod_{u\le t}R(\omega\Delta_u) = R(\omega\sum_{u\le t}\Delta_u)$. That collapse is
what buys the parallel prefix scan.

A convolution with $K>1$ makes a token's increment depend on its neighbours, so the
phase stops being a function of the multiset and the map from action sequences to
$SO(2)^{\nb}$ is no longer a homomorphism. Commutativity is the central
computational property here, and it is also the stated limitation: a turn/forward
action space, where displacement depends on accumulated heading, is not
representable by a fixed per-token increment.


\section{The magnitude axis}
\label{sec:pope}

The other factor of \eqref{eq:polar}. Three occupants, differing in whether content
enters instantaneously or by accumulation.

\paragraph{PoPE} (Gopalakrishnan et al., 2025) is defined by \emph{deleting} a term.
RoPE's real-valued $q$ carries an implicit intrinsic phase $\psi^q$, and
\eqref{eq:polar} shows it entering the cosine alongside the positional angle ---
what PoPE calls an entanglement of ``what'' with ``where''. PoPE removes the
interaction $\psi^k - \psi^q$ and keeps only a content-dependent \emph{magnitude},
$\mu = \softplus(\cdot) \ge 0$, with the phase a pure index clock plus a learned
per-(head, frequency) constant $\delta_c$. It is the exact complement of MapFormer,
which amplifies the same phase term into a learned accumulator.

\paragraph{Forget gates.} FoX computes a scalar $f_t = \sigma(w_f^\top x_t + b_f)$
per head and adds $\sum_{j<\ell\le t}\log f_\ell$ to the logit. Gated linear RNNs
(GLA, Mamba, Mamba-2) do the same thing inside a recurrence,
$S_t = \operatorname{Diag}(\alpha_t)S_{t-1} + k_tv_t$. GRAPE proves FoX is an exact
instance of its additive family. All are accumulated content-dependent magnitude,
with $\log\gamma < 0$ by construction.

\paragraph{ALiBi} is the index-driven, content-free member: a linear-in-lag penalty,
realisable by a defective generator.


\section{One equation}
\label{sec:unify}

The two axes are disjoint coordinates of a single object. Put each token's
contribution in \emph{log}-polar form. Let $c^q, c^k, G : \R^d \to \C^{\nb}$ and set
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:unified}
  \boxed{\;
  \tilde q_t = \exp\!\big(c^q(x_t) - \overline{S_t}\big),\qquad
  \tilde k_s = \exp\!\big(c^k(x_s) + S_s\big),\qquad
  S_t = \textstyle\sum_{u\le t} G(x_u),}
\end{equation}
componentwise, with $a_{ts} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_c
\operatorname{Re}[\overline{\tilde q_t^{(c)}}\,\tilde k_s^{(c)}]$. Writing
$c = \gamma + i\pi$ and $G = \delta + i\eta$ with all four real,
\begin{equation}
  a_{ts} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_c
  \underbrace{e^{\,\gamma^q_{t,c}+\gamma^k_{s,c}+\sum_{t<u\le s}\delta_{u,c}}}_{\text{magnitude}}
  \cos\Big(\underbrace{\pi^k_{s,c}-\pi^q_{t,c}+\textstyle\sum_{t<u\le s}\eta_{u,c}}_{\text{phase}}\Big).
\end{equation}

\noindent $c$ is the instantaneous complex log and $G$ the accumulated one; real
parts are magnitude, imaginary parts are phase. Every mechanism in
$\S\S$\ref{sec:phase}--\ref{sec:pope} is a choice of which of the four real fields
$\gamma, \pi, \delta, \eta$ is non-trivial.

\paragraph{The conjugate is load-bearing, and the two accumulators carry opposite
signs.} The Hermitian form $\overline{\tilde q}\,\tilde k$ \emph{subtracts} phases
and \emph{adds} log-magnitudes. So $\operatorname{Im}G$ must be carried with the
\emph{same} sign on $q$ and $k$ to yield $\sum_{t<u\le s}\eta_u$, while
$\operatorname{Re}G$ must be carried \emph{oppositely} to yield
$\sum_{t<u\le s}\delta_u$; carried with the same sign it would give
$\sum_{u\le t} + \sum_{u\le s}$, a joint scale carrying no information about
separation. Perturbing the prefix $u\le t$ and asking whether the score moves --- a
relative score must not:

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\small
\caption{Only one of the four sign combinations is interval-relative. The prefix $u\le t$ is perturbed and the score must not move.}
\label{tab:signs}
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
$\operatorname{Re}G$ & $\operatorname{Im}G$ & relative prefix leak \\
\midrule
\textbf{opposed} & \textbf{aligned} & $\mathbf{3.4\times10^{-16}}$ \\
opposed & opposed & $1.4$ \\
aligned & aligned & $3.1$ \\
aligned & opposed & $7.1$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent Exactly one of the four combinations is interval-relative, and it is the
one \eqref{eq:unified} writes. With $\delta = \log\gamma < 0$ that slot \emph{is} the
forget gate; with $\eta$ content-dependent the phase slot is MapFormer and Selective
RoPE. \textbf{MapPoPE is not a third mechanism} --- PoPE constrains $c$, the phase
family constrains $\operatorname{Im}G$, the coordinates are disjoint, so imposing
both is an immediate consequence rather than a design.

\paragraph{Interval-invariance is the right notion of ``relative''.} With
content-dependent increments the logit is not a function of $s-t$ and the score
matrix is not Toeplitz. The property that survives, and the one that matters, is
that the score depends on the tokens in $(t,s]$ and not on the prefix.



\section{A map or a clock: what cancellation chooses}
\label{sec:clockmap}

It is tempting to read a signed increment as simply better. It is not, and the
reason decides which mechanism a task wants.

Attention sees only the interval sum
$\theta_s - \theta_t = \omega\sum_{t<u\le s}\Delta_u$. What that quantity
\emph{is} depends entirely on whether the increments can cancel:

injective in & \textbf{space}, blind to time & \textbf{time}, blind to space \\[2pt]

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent These are mutually exclusive by construction, and each is \emph{correct}
for one job. A cognitive map \textbf{needs} same-place-different-time to collide ---
that collision is exactly what revisit prediction asks for. Language needs the
opposite: a code that made token $5$ and token $15$ interchangeable because some
``backward'' token intervened would be broken, not economical.

So the monotone increment of CARoPE, GRAPE-AP and CoPE is not an oversight that the
state-tracking literature caught them in. \textbf{On language a monotone clock is the
right object}, which is why RoPE --- $\Delta\equiv1$, maximally monotone --- is the
sensible default there and why nothing went visibly wrong. The limitation appears
only where the task demands a quantity that is invariant to route, which on text is
essentially parity and formal-language state tracking, and on navigation is
everything.

\paragraph{The growth exponent is a readout of which question, not a score.}
$\alpha\approx1$ means the accumulator is measuring elapsed time, which is unbounded,
so the code must eventually leave any range it was trained on. $\alpha\approx0.5$
means it is measuring a random-walk position. The ideal for a bounded map would be
$\alpha = 0$ --- position on a torus is bounded --- and nothing here achieves it,
because every mechanism accumulates without wrapping. That is a design gap, not a
property of the frame.

\paragraph{What MapFormer gave up to get the map.} A signed $\Delta$ makes two
visits to the same cell positionally identical. That is the point, and it also means
the phase cannot represent \emph{how long ago}. Anything recency-dependent has to get
that information from somewhere else.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Which is a candidate account of the forget gate, whose mechanism is otherwise
unaccounted for.} (The measurements cited here are set out with their protocol in
the companion paper; ``unmeasured'' there has the specific sense of a contrast
inside its minimum detectable effect.) It adds a second, content-dependent, \emph{monotone}
accumulator in the magnitude slot --- measured at $\alpha = +0.956$, ballistic,
beside the phase accumulator's $0.6$ --- so it restores the clock that the signed
phase gave up. Every otherwise-puzzling detail follows: it needs a \emph{live}
$\lambda$ (frozen at zero it lands on the baseline, $-0.016$, unmeasured) but not
\emph{decay}; $5$ of $8$ seeds learn $\lambda<0$, and those gain most
($r(\lambda,\text{gain}) = -0.516$). A negative $\lambda$ still gives a monotone
accumulator, counting up instead of down. On this reading it was never a forget gate
--- it is a clock, and the sign of $\lambda$ only sets which way it runs.

\textbf{Predicted, and untested:} any monotone content-dependent increment of either
sign should reproduce the gain; a content-\emph{independent} constant increment (a
pure index clock) should reproduce much of it; and the gain should vanish on a task
with no recency structure.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{This is now tested directly, and it holds.} Until recently the
dichotomy rested on the construction above plus a correlation across four arms
(the companion paper); every task measured here wanted a \emph{map}, so
``signed wins'' and ``signed suits this task'' were not separable. On a task built
to want a \emph{clock}, the ordering inverts as predicted: constraining the
increment to be monotone costs $0.215$--$0.280$ on the torus and nothing
measurable there, and the same unconstrained architecture learns
$\alpha = 0.591$ on one and $0.967$ on the other. the companion paper.

\noindent Read this way the frame's two slots are not two knobs but \textbf{two
answers}: a signed phase is a map, a monotone accumulated magnitude is a clock, and a
system that needs both must spend both slots. Mamba-3 claiming both deliberately is
then the natural endpoint rather than a curiosity. In softmax attention the
combination is so far only partial: MapPoPE pairs a content-dependent phase with a
content-dependent magnitude that is \emph{instantaneous}, so it supplies no clock at
all. A content-dependent phase alongside an \emph{accumulated} content-dependent
magnitude, in softmax attention, is the configuration nothing in the table
occupies.



\section{The relational map}
\label{sec:map}

Eight axes separate everything in the arena. They are not independent, but no two
mechanisms agree on all eight.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt

\item[\textbf{A4}] \textbf{Algebra} --- semisimple diagonal ($\C^\times$), unipotent,

\item[\textbf{A5}] \textbf{Sign} --- may the increment be negative?
\item[\textbf{A6}] \textbf{Rank} of the content-to-increment map.
\item[\textbf{A7}] \textbf{Host} --- softmax attention, or linear attention / SSM.
\item[\textbf{A8}] \textbf{Composition} --- parallel prefix sum, path-ordered

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Non-abelian transport: PaTH, DeltaNet, RWKV-7.} The interval operator
becomes a cumulative \emph{product} of Householder-like reflections
$I-\beta_t w_tw_t^{\!\top}$ rather than a sum of increments. PaTH proves this takes
attention beyond $TC^0$ (on the assumption $TC^0 \neq NC^1$); DeltaNet contributes the
chunkwise-parallel algorithm that makes the product trainable at scale; RWKV-7
generalises the rule to vector-valued gating and shows it recognises \emph{all}
regular languages with a constant number of layers. The price is that no $A(d)$ and no
$S_t$ exist.

\paragraph{Pair-dependence: CoPE.} Position stops being a property of a token. A gate
is computed for every query--key \emph{pair},
\[
  g_{ij} = \sigma(q_i^{\!\top}k_j), \qquad p_{ij} = \sum_{k=j}^{i} g_{ik},
\]
so the distance from $i$ to $j$ counts only the tokens the query elects to count. The
paper's own example states the idea most clearly: to measure position in
\emph{sentences}, the gate need only fire on sentence separators. Position becomes
query-dependent and denominated in task-relevant units, which is exactly why no
per-token $\theta_t$ exists and why CoPE cannot be written in \eqref{eq:gen}. It
solves selective copying, counting and Flip-Flop, on which standard encodings fail,
and improves perplexity on language and code. It is also monotone ($\sigma>0$), so
$\S$\ref{sec:clockmap} applies: CoPE builds a better \emph{clock}, one whose tick is
chosen by content, and not a map.

DAPE leaves by the same door and one more. It computes an additive bias as
$\mathrm{MLP}(a_{ij}, b_{ij})$ from the attention score and a fixed positional prior,
so it too is a function of the \emph{pair}, and the MLP costs linearity as well. It
is worth naming because of when it appeared: DAPE and CoPE are both 2024, and they
made position content-dependent on the \textbf{additive} side more than a year before
CARoPE, Selective RoPE, MapFormer and Mamba-3 did the same on the multiplicative
side.

\paragraph{Broken path structure: MesaNet, Titans, TAPE.} These give up
increment-accumulation itself. MesaNet solves a local least-squares problem to
optimality at every timestep by conjugate gradient --- a prefix-global inverse rather
than a running sum --- and reports lower perplexity than Mamba-2 and other modern RNNs
at matched scale while remaining behind transformers on in-context recall, which is
the primal/dual trade-off of $\S$\ref{sec:memory} showing up as a benchmark result.
Titans replaces the linear state with a deep memory module trained at test time by
gradient descent with momentum and weight decay, and scales past $2$M-token
needle-in-a-haystack. TAPE keeps a positional \emph{tensor} and updates it across
layers through attention and MLP blocks under permutation- and
orthogonal-equivariance constraints, improving long-sequence perplexity and
length-generalised addition. In none of the three is the operator applied to a stored
trace a function of the interval, so the classification does not reach them.

\paragraph{Broken linearity and commutativity: TEM-t.} Treated in
$\S$\ref{sec:emtem}, where the two independent breaks are separated.

\subsection{Reading the map}

\emph{(i) The frame's own cells are full.} Crossing A1 (magnitude / phase) with A3
(index / content) gives four cells and all four are occupied: RoPE, ALiBi, Mamba-3's
rotation, FoX. GRAPE holds the index-multiplicative and content-additive cells;
Mamba-3, Selective RoPE and CARoPE hold content-phase. The remaining structure is
\textbf{content-dependent, signed, orthogonal and still abelian} --- contextual
multiplicative --- which is where MapFormer, Selective RoPE and Mamba-3 sit.
CARoPE occupies the same cell on every axis but the sign, where its increment is
constrained to $(0,1)$; $\S$\ref{sec:clockmap} is about what that costs.

\emph{(ii) A5 is settled next door and unapplied here.} Sarrof et al.\ (2024)
observed that SSM gates are ``always nonnegative \dots\ due to exponential or sigmoid
parameterizations'', with a theorem: such models cannot recognise \textsc{parity}.
Grazzi et al.\ (ICLR 2025) proved the eigenvalue version and fixed it by extending
the range to $[-1,1]$; PaTH adopts it deliberately; RWKV-7 restricts it only for
training stability; and Selective RoPE's $\S$4.2 already demonstrates single-layer
Transformer parity from rotations that ``model \emph{flips}''. Yet CARoPE, GRAPE-AP
and CoPE are all monotone --- by softplus, by construction, by sigmoid --- and none
cites any of that literature. the companion paper measures what the constraint costs
where it can be seen.

\emph{(iii) A6 is thinner than a blank column suggests.} Two rows constrain something
rank-like for reasons of their own: PaTH's Householder factors are $I + $rank-$1$ by
definition, and CARoPE emits one scalar per head, putting its content-to-angle map
\emph{below} MapFormer's $r$ rather than above it. The nearest deliberate study is
LieRE's generator tile-size sweep, which varies density on the \emph{output} side and
peaks in the interior at $8\times8$. What no one sweeps is the \emph{input-side}
bottleneck: how many dimensions of content may steer the angle.

\emph{(iv) A7 is nearly free.} Selective RoPE runs in both hosts and Mamba-3 crosses
over by state-space duality, so ``softmax versus SSM'' separates implementations
rather than mechanisms.






\section{Where MapEM and TEM-t sit}
\label{sec:emtem}

Two mechanisms from the cognitive-map literature are placeable, and the placements
are opposite.

\paragraph{MapEM lifts the frame rather than leaving it.} MapEM computes two
attention matrices and multiplies them elementwise,
$A = \softmax(A_X \odot A_P)$. With $(A_X)_{ts} = q^x_t\!\cdot\!k^x_s$ and
$(A_P)_{ts} = q^p_t\!\cdot\!k^p_s$,
\[
  (A_X \odot A_P)_{ts} \;=\; \big(q^x_t \otimes q^p_t\big)\cdot\big(k^x_s \otimes k^p_s\big),
\]
so the Hadamard product \emph{is} a single bilinear form --- on the tensor product of
the content and position spaces, of dimension $d_xd_p$ rather than $d$ (verified to
$1.9\times10^{-6}$ in float32). Everything in $\S$\ref{sec:unify} applies with $c$ and
$G$ valued in $\C^{d_xd_p}$.

That is sharper than ``an AND-gate rather than an OR-gate'', and it says what the lift
costs: a rank-one tensor $q^x\otimes q^p$ can express content-\emph{and}-position but
not content-\emph{or}-position, and there is no fallback channel when either factor is
uninformative. It predicts the observed sensitivity to how the position channel is
initialised --- a single shared $p_0$ beats separate $q^p_0, k^p_0$ by $+0.358$ on a
matching task ($3/3$ seeds) --- because with no additive fallback a mis-set position
factor multiplies the whole score instead of being outvoted.

\paragraph{TEM-t is the same machine with two conditions removed.} TEM-t
(Whittington et al., 2022) generates position recurrently,
$e_t = \sigma(e_{t-1}W_{a_t})$ with $\sigma$ a ReLU and $W_a$ a general per-action
matrix, then sets $Q = K = W_e e$ and $V = W_x x$; content is absent from the logit
entirely and enters only through the values.

Its logit is $e_t^\top W_e^\top W_e\,e_s$. Writing $e_s = W_{t\to s}e_t$ and
$e_t = W_{0\to t}e_0$, with $W_e$ orthogonal this is
$e_0^\top W_{0\to t}^{\top}W_{t\to s}W_{0\to t}e_0$ --- \textbf{the interval operator
conjugated by the prefix}. Conjugation is trivial exactly when the group is abelian:

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\small
\caption{TEM-t's logit is interval-relative only when the transition group is abelian and there is no nonlinearity --- which is MapFormer's configuration.}
\label{tab:tem}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
transition group & logit depends on the interval alone? \\
\midrule
abelian (block rotations), no $\sigma$ & \textbf{yes} \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent \textbf{MapFormer is precisely the surviving row}: $SO(2)^{\nb}$ is abelian
by construction and there is no nonlinearity on the path. TEM-t breaks both, and
either break alone suffices.

This is the mechanical account of a large empirical cost. No group law means no
$S_t$, hence no parallel prefix scan, hence a sequential loop: at $L=2048$ a faithful
TEM is $120\times$ slower than at $L=128$ and $1632\times$ slower in absolute terms
than a MapFormer of $20\times$ \emph{more} parameters. The constant includes
interpreter overhead; the shape is the missing group law. TEM-t is 2022 and MapFormer
2025, so on this axis the later contribution is the \emph{restriction} --- abelian and
linear --- that buys the scan and the relative law.


\section{The memory reading: what the frame looks like from the other side}
\label{sec:memory}

A large literature --- fast weight programmers, linear attention, gated linear
RNNs, state-space models and their hybrids --- studies the same equation while
asking a different question. It is worth stating the correspondence, because the
two literatures partition the design space almost perfectly and neither says so.

\paragraph{One primitive, two forms.} The outer product $v_u \otimes k_u$ binds a
value to a key, and superposing bindings gives an associative memory read by a
query. The object appears as Kohonen's correlation-matrix memory (1972),
Schmidhuber's fast weight programmer (1992), the recurrent form of linear attention
(Katharopoulos et al., 2020), the hidden state of a scalar-$A$ state-space model,
Smolensky's tensor-product role--filler binding (1990), and --- via the dual form of
Irie et al.\ (2022) --- the weight matrix of any linearly-parameterised layer trained
by gradient descent. Schlag et al.\ (2021) made the linear-attention correspondence
explicit. This gives every mechanism two readings:
\[
  \underbrace{W_t = \textstyle\sum_{u\le t} A(t-u)\,v_u k_u^{\!\top},\quad y_t = W_tq_t}_{\text{\emph{primal}: compress, bounded state}}
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  \underbrace{a_{ts} = \tilde q_t\!\cdot\!\tilde k_s \text{ over kept items}}_{\text{\emph{dual}: keep and attend}}
\]

\paragraph{The positional operator is the memory's transport kernel.} $A(d)$ is what
happens to a stored trace as it ages $d$ steps, and $\S$\ref{sec:classification}
says there are two things it can do:
\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\small
\caption{The two slots, read as a memory. $A(d)$ is the transport kernel applied to a stored trace as it ages.}
\label{tab:memory}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
slot & positional reading & memory reading \\
\midrule
$\operatorname{Re}G$ & magnitude / decay & \textbf{how fast the trace is forgotten} \\
$\operatorname{Im}G$ & phase / rotation  & \textbf{how the trace is re-addressed as it ages} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent It also identifies A8 with the primal/dual choice: \textbf{a positional
mechanism admits a parallel prefix scan exactly when the memory admits a bounded
recurrent state}, because both are the group law $A(d)A(e) = A(d+e)$. That is one
condition, not two.

\paragraph{The two literatures have divided the slots without noticing.} The
fast-weight literature owns $\operatorname{Re}G$ exhaustively. Irie and Gershman's
review (TMLR 2025) categorises the whole modern family by \emph{decay type} ---
RetNet a constant scalar, Mamba-2 a scalar, GLA a rate per row of the fast weight
--- and the words ``rotary'', ``RoPE'' and ``positional'' do not occur in it. The
positional-encoding literature owns $\operatorname{Im}G$ and treats decay as a
side-condition. Mamba-3 is the first design to claim both deliberately, and it
arrives from the memory side, stating the crossing as a proposition on
``data-dependent RoPE equivalence''. The hybrid quadratic-linear transformers of
Irie et al.\ (2025) use RoPE, but only as an off-the-shelf component inside the
attention half rather than as a design axis.

\paragraph{Positional mechanisms are key-side by construction, which makes the frame
a theory of addressing.} Gershman, Fiete and Irie (2025) argue that what
distinguishes key--value memory from classical similarity-based retrieval is that it
holds \emph{separate} representations for storage and for retrieval, so the two can
be optimised independently --- fidelity for values, discriminability for keys --- and
that the binding constraint on memory performance is addressing rather than capacity.

Every mechanism in Table~\ref{tab:map} acts on $q$ and $k$ and none of them touches $v$. Read
through the correspondence above that is not a convention: \textbf{a positional
mechanism is an addressing rule}, and $A(d)$ says how the address of a trace changes
as it ages. The two slots are then two ways for an address to move:
$\operatorname{Im}G$ transports it around a torus, so a trace becomes retrievable
again at a predictable displacement; $\operatorname{Re}G$ makes it fade, so the trace
becomes unretrievable at a rate the content chooses. Nothing in the frame can alter
what was stored, only where it can be found.

\paragraph{Why decay is compulsory on one side of the line and optional on the
other.} A visible asymmetry in Table~\ref{tab:map} is that every SSM row carries
$\operatorname{Re}G < 0$ while most softmax rows leave the magnitude slot empty.
Schlag et al.\ (2021) supply the reason: an additive outer-product memory has a
finite \emph{capacity}, and once the sequence length exceeds it the model enters an
overcapacity regime in which stored associations interfere. In the primal form decay
is therefore capacity management and not a modelling preference --- something has to
evict. In the dual form the items are kept separately and the softmax renormalises,
so nothing saturates and a decay term (ALiBi, FoX) is an inductive bias one may
decline. The same equation, and the slot is mandatory on one side and free on the
other.

That also sharpens what the delta rule is for. Schlag et al.\ introduce it precisely
because purely additive updates cannot \emph{edit} a stale association, only bury it;
the rewrite is a capacity fix before it is an expressivity fix, and the expressivity
--- state tracking, parity --- comes along with it.

\paragraph{The non-abelian family is the rewritable-memory family.} The first row of
the out-of-frame table is not a collection of exceptions. DeltaNet, PaTH and RWKV-7
replace the \emph{additive} Hebbian update with a \emph{delta rule},
\[
  W_t = W_{t-1} + \sigma(\beta_t)\big(v_t - W_{t-1}\phi(k_t)\big)\otimes\phi(k_t),
\]
whose state-transition matrix $I - \sigma(\beta_t)\phi\phi^{\!\top}$ is a
Householder-like reflection rather than a scalar. Memory becomes rewritable ---
stale associations are corrected instead of accumulated --- and the price is exactly
the group law: reflections do not commute, so no $A(d)$ and no $S_t$ exist and the
composition is a path-ordered product. \textbf{What that family trades away is the
positional structure, and what it buys is a memory that can overwrite.} It is also
where the sign axis of $\S$\ref{sec:map}(ii) is deliberately exercised: with
$\sigma = 2\,\mathrm{sigmoid}$ the eigenvalues lie in $(-1,1)$ \emph{including
negative values}, which is what unlocks parity and modular state tracking.

\paragraph{And MapEM's lift is a binding.} The tensor product
$q^x \otimes q^p$ of $\S$\ref{sec:emtem} is Smolensky's role--filler binding: content
as filler, position as role. Read from the memory side, MapEM stores each
observation bound to its place rather than superposing the two channels additively
--- which is the same trade the primal/dual axis makes, one level down.

\medskip
\noindent This is a change of reading, not of mechanism. It says where to look for the unoccupied designs: the cells that are
empty in one literature are usually the ones the other has already filled.


\section{The readout axis}
\label{sec:readout}

$\S$\ref{sec:phase} treats the readout $A = \operatorname{diag}(\omega)$ as fixed.
A large and practically important literature varies exactly that, and it is disjoint
from everything above: Position Interpolation, RoPE scaling laws, NTK-aware scaling,
Dynamic NTK, NTK-by-parts, YaRN, LongRoPE and LongRoPE2 all rescale the frequency
ladder so a model trained at one length runs at another. Every one of them is
index-driven, so on Table~\ref{tab:map} they occupy a single cell --- but they vary something the
eight axes do not name.

\paragraph{The critical dimension.} The argument that organises that line is worth
importing. Channel $r$ has wavelength $T_r = 2\pi b^{2r/\hd}$. High-frequency
channels complete many cycles inside the training length $L_0$ and are trained
through their whole phase range; low-frequency channels may not complete even one.
The boundary
\[
  r_{\mathrm{crit}} \;=\; \min\{\,r : T_r \ge L_0\,\}
\]
separates them, and beyond the training length the channels past it enter phase
intervals never seen in training. For LLaMA-2 ($\hd = 128$, $L_0 = 4096$) that is
$36$ of $128$ dimensions. This is a mechanism for length-extrapolation failure that
is about \emph{which channels were trained}, not about capacity or precision.



\section{Choosing a mechanism}
\label{sec:capability}

What
a task demands of a positional code, which mechanisms supply it, and
which trade-offs are forced rather than incidental.

\subsection{Three hard trade-offs}

\paragraph{(T1) Commutativity buys the scan and costs path-dependence.} With $K=1$
and a linear increment the phase depends on the multiset of tokens traversed, the
prefix product collapses to a prefix sum, and inference is $O(\log T)$ parallel. The
same property makes displacement-under-accumulated-heading unrepresentable: a fixed
per-token increment cannot express ``forward'' when forward depends on where you are
facing. Measured both ways --- the position effect falls from $+0.438$ to $+0.050$
under turn/turn/forward actions, and a mechanism that abandons the group law pays for
it in the scan ($\S$\ref{sec:emtem}). You may have one or the other.

\paragraph{(T2) A signed increment buys a map and gives up a clock.} The accumulator's
growth exponent is set by how completely opposite actions cancel, but the
exponent is a symptom and the substance is $\S$\ref{sec:clockmap}:
a signed increment makes the interval sum a \emph{net displacement}, invariant to
route and blind to elapsed time, and a monotone one makes it a \emph{path length},
injective in time and blind to place. Neither dominates. On navigation the signed
form wins outright (at matched training loss the monotone one does not beat a plain
index clock anywhere); on language the monotone form is the correct object, and the
signed one would be discarding the very distinction position exists to make.

\paragraph{(T3) Compressing the memory makes decay compulsory; rewriting it forfeits
T1.} Both halves are argued in $\S$\ref{sec:memory}: the primal form saturates so
something must evict, while the dual form renormalises and may decline a decay term;
and a memory that must \emph{overwrite} needs a delta rule, whose transition matrix is
a reflection rather than a scalar.

\subsection{What follows for choosing a model}

\emph{On language, most of this is invisible}, for the reason
$\S$\ref{sec:clockmap} gives: order is nearly all that is demanded, and a clock is the
right object for it. The exception is worth naming, because it is the one place a
text task makes a sharp demand: \emph{contextual} counting --- the $k$-th most
recent item of some selected class --- is a clock a fixed index cannot supply, and
the gap there ($+0.750$, the companion paper) is larger than anything measured
on navigation. It is also the demand CoPE was built for.

\emph{On navigation and any task with a metric structure, the sign and the
conditioning of the increment are the whole game.} They are worth more per parameter than anything
else measured here, and both dominate the choice of positional \emph{encoding}
(the companion paper).

\emph{One combination is ruled out rather than merely absent.} A mechanism with a
bounded recurrent state, signed transport \emph{and} rewritable memory does not exist,
and T1 says the last two cannot both be had while keeping the parallel scan.


\section{Conclusion}

Three assumptions --- linearity, translation invariance, continuity --- reduce the
space of positional mechanisms to the classification of a single generator, and the
generator has exactly two live parts. Everything in the arena is a choice about
where content enters those two parts, and everything that appears to escape does so
by giving up one of the three assumptions, at a price that can be named: the
classical relative methods give up continuity and with it any extrapolation; the
delta-rule family gives up commutativity and with it the parallel scan; CoPE and DAPE
give up per-token factorisation and with it any $\theta_t$ at all; and MesaNet,
Titans and TAPE give up the accumulation structure itself.

The two parts are not two knobs. A signed accumulated phase is a map and a monotone
one is a clock, and a system that needs both must spend both slots. That is why the
monotone increments of CARoPE, GRAPE-AP and CoPE are correct on language and fail on
navigation, and why the sign of the increment --- which costs nothing, and which no
paper in the positional-encoding literature varies deliberately --- is worth more
than any other ingredient measured here. The dichotomy is not only an
interpretation: on a task whose answer depends on elapsed count rather than net
displacement the cost of forcing a monotone increment falls from $0.28$ to nothing
measurable, and an architecture free to choose picks a different accumulator for
each task (the companion paper). The corollary for practice is that a
mechanism does not have a quality, it has a match: what to spend on is decided by
the task.

What the frame does not yet explain is the axis on which several mechanisms
independently help: extrapolated length. The accumulator's growth law accounts for
two of them and demonstrably not for the others --- and no mechanism in the table
bounds the accumulator at all, including the one that was assumed to. Nor is the
growth law itself yet a cause: $\theta$ is periodic, so a larger accumulator is not
obviously out of distribution, and the one published account of why it would be is
refuted here. $\alpha$ unifies two results and reads off what a task demanded; it
does not yet explain either. That is the gap worth building into.


\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{The input-side rank.} $r=2$ is the worst setting and $r=4$ fixes it for
  $384$ parameters, but nothing explains why the optimiser finds a skewed basis at
  $r=2$ and a well-conditioned one at $r=4$ when the learned solution occupies a
  $2$-plane either way.
\item \textbf{The OOD-length axis, still the largest gap.} The accumulator's growth
  exponent accounts for the sign and rank results together (the companion paper),
  and it predicts that bounding the accumulator should help at length. The obvious
  candidate for something that bounds it --- the wrapped innovation of an
  InEKF-style correction --- does not: it wraps the innovation and not the state,
  and the accumulator's range is unchanged ($285.6$ against $283.9$). Measured,
  neither the forget gate nor PoPE changes $\alpha$ either (both inside their MDEs),
  so at least one other cause is at work on an axis where four separate mechanisms
  help. $\S$\ref{sec:clockmap} proposes an account for the forget gate --- it
  restores the clock a signed phase gives up --- of which one prediction is now
  cheap to run, since the companion paper supplies a task with explicit recency
  structure.

  \textbf{The obvious test of the growth law is malformed, and the real gap is
  prior to it.} Varying $\alpha$ deliberately and checking that degradation follows
  cannot be done: $\alpha$ is not a parameter of any model here --- it is a
  statistic fitted to a trained one --- and the two ways to move it both fail.
  Interpolating
  from a signed to a monotone increment moves $\alpha$ but destroys the map at the
  same time, so on a map task it isolates nothing beyond the sign ablation; and
  bounding the accumulator is either a no-op, since $\theta$ enters through
  $\cos,\sin$ and $\theta$ and $\theta+2\pi$ are already identical, or it breaks
  additivity and with it the interval-relativity that the whole frame rests on.
  Within this frame $\alpha$ appears not to be independently controllable: it is
  set by the cancellation, and the only lever on the cancellation is A5 and A6.
  That would make $\alpha$ a \emph{re-description} of those two axes rather than a
  separate cause --- which is consistent with its covering exactly those two
  mechanisms and none of the other four.

  \textbf{The prior question is why a growing accumulator should cost anything at
  all}, given that the code is periodic. The natural answer --- that low-frequency
  channels reach phases never trained --- is the long-context literature's
  critical-dimension account, and it was imported and \textbf{refuted}
  (the companion paper): ablating those channels costs \emph{more} at
  extrapolated length, not less. So $\alpha$'s association with OOD degradation is
  established and its route is not. Until that is answered, $\alpha$
  \emph{unifies} the sign and rank results and \emph{diagnoses} what a task
  demanded, and it does not explain.
\item \textbf{Defective generators.} Jordan-RoPE occupies the cell; what polynomial
  factors in the lag are \emph{for} remains open, and no navigation evaluation exists.
\item \textbf{NoPE.} A causal decoder learns an implicit position without any operator
  at all, and in at least one controlled ablation it has the best perplexity of every
  encoding tested (Table~\ref{tab:reported}). The frame classifies operators and has
  nothing to say about the case where the right operator is the identity; when an
  encoding helps on text, how much of the gain is over NoPE rather than over RoPE is
  usually not reported.
\item \textbf{Sign on language.} A monotone clock cannot represent a $-1$ action, and
  on navigation that costs everything. On text HGRN's own ablation finds a
  data-dependent phase worse than a constant one. Whether the sign matters there, and
  for what, is untested --- and the companion paper sharpens rather than settles
  it: on a symbolic clock task the sign is free to be given up at no cost, which is
  consistent with text needing a clock, but the arms there are trained from scratch
  on one synthetic stream. The published dataset that would settle it is
  Flip-Flop LM, which is that task at $k=1$.
\item \textbf{Non-commuting action spaces.} Rotation-based actions defeat a fixed
  per-token increment. Allocentric recoding sidesteps it wherever heading is known;
  the non-abelian mechanisms (PaTH, DeltaNet, RWKV-7) address it directly and have
  never been evaluated on navigation.
\end{itemize}
