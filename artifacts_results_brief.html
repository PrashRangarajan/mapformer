Cognitive-map sequence models · results brief

What makes an in-context cognitive map work — and where each ingredient earns its keep

A faithful reproduction of MapFormer (Rambaud et al., 2025) plus a factorial study across four axes — position code, positional encoding, time-hierarchy, and backbone — on two grid environments, extended into continuous 3D (§08) and now onto the looped-transformer literature’s own benchmarks (§1c). The through-line: no single ingredient wins everywhere; the environment decides which one to spend on — and as of this revision we know which property of the environment does the deciding. Two ingredients survive everything: path integration costs 448 parameters and buys +0.078 on parity; recursion costs none and buys +0.056. Ten earlier claims were overturned in the process; they are listed first, not buried.

The one-line result

Two ingredients survive everything, they are nearly free, and they add. Path integration is what makes an in-context cognitive map work at all — an index model sits on the floor where a path-integrated one solves the task. Weight-shared recursion buys the depth those tasks need at a third of the parameters. Stacked, they cost 448 parameters and beat a model three times the size.

The one-line caveat

Which ingredient to spend on is decided by the environment, not by the architecture. Path integration is worth 40× the encoding choice on a torus and is negative on rotation-action MiniGrid — a mismatch that is removable by recording absolute displacement, but only if you know to. There is no default that wins everywhere.

What makes these numbers worth showing. Every table states its own measured floor, which ranges from 0.06 to 0.80 across tasks. Every cross-model comparison is trained in one batch, seed-matched and parameter-matched. Every new task is gated for trivial shortcuts — n-gram baselines on the answer stream, and a context-destruction ablation — before any GPU time is spent; four earlier tasks in this project were voided by exactly those checks after the fact. Effects are reported against a minimum detectable effect, so “unmeasured” and “null” stay distinct.

The common cause. Training outcomes in this regime are bimodal — grid 8 lands at 0.448 or 0.990; a three-layer arm at 0.14 or 0.81; a looped arm at 0.12 or 0.77. Every method that assumes a point estimate breaks on that. Worse, retraining with identical seeds and settings does not reproduce (mean per-seed drift 0.185), so the seed does not index the basin. What has to be measured is the failure rate, not the mean — and n must be large enough to estimate a distribution.

Why the sign flips

Of five properties differing between the environments, rotation-based actions account for −0.388 of the −0.438 swing (n=8) — more than the other four combined. MiniGrid's turn/forward actions break MapFormer's fixed-per-token delta; the torus uses pure translations.

The remedy

Recording the action stream as absolute displacement instead of turn/forward restores the effect completely at 4 headings (+0.050 → +0.488) and at 12 (+0.26 to +0.38). Measured in a synthetic rotation env at Habitat's 30° turn granularity — not real Habitat. No architecture change.

With a world-fixed action encoding, path integration is the dominant ingredient in both environments (§01). The MiniGrid reversal is driven by raw rotation actions, not the environment itself — on MiniGrid the two best of eight arms are index-position models, but that ranking is a property of the action encoding, which is a free choice.

Mechanism · what triggers the effect

It is map size, and it is a threshold

Four candidate explanations were tested against each other by holding one constant and varying the others. Only map extent survives. Aliasing survives too — with the opposite sign to what was claimed.

Why aliasing runs backwards

Distinctive observations do not substitute for position — they raise the precision demand. Knowing what sits at cells you have already seen never tells you which cell you are standing on; with 32 cells sharing a token an approximate fix still earns partial credit, with 2 it does not.

What the budget was worth

At 400 epochs the index arm was flat in 1/5 seeds and the effect read +0.374. Doubling to 800 converged it 3/3 and the effect fell to +0.305. A convergence-conditioning argument had predicted the opposite direction — trust budget extensions over conditioning arguments.

Structural limit, worth knowing before designing here. Prior-visit counts per grid size do not overlap (grid 8 spans 5.67–18.35 across episode lengths; grid 32 spans 1.95–4.13). Small maps force frequent revisits, so map extent and visit statistics are near-inseparable in this environment at any episode length. The separation above comes from pooling conditions where episode length varies at fixed map size; confirming it needs an environment where the two are independently settable.

Refining the position estimate buys nothing null

A loop that carries and corrects θ each pass — this project's Kalman work moved to the depth axis — adds +0.090 (t=1.56). The learned gate settles at mean |g| 0.083 with inconsistent sign across seeds, capping the correction at 0.14 rad against a θ spanning ~2πT. The gate was verifiably escapable; the optimiser simply declined to refine.

What that extends

The standing finding on the sequence axis was that the Kalman win came from stabilisation and token-type gating, not inference. The depth axis now says the same thing by an independent route: the loop's benefit is iteration, not a better position estimate.

Recursion buys parameters, not compute — four passes cost four passes, measured. It does not move the wall: every index configuration still collapses past interval ~32 while a 204K one-layer path-integrated model holds 0.945 at 65+. And it is not this project's idea — "recursion substitutes for depth" is the premise of the Recursive-Transformer line (ALBERT; Mixture-of-Recursions, NeurIPS 2025), which our index-arm result replicates rather than establishes.
The loop's margin shrinks whenever the baseline is trained better. On Match-Query it went +0.373 → +0.146 between two recipes, because the better recipe gained the baseline +0.139 while the loop lost 0.089. On the clean torus its training-length win is +0.052 raw and +0.006 loss-matched. Much of what looks like capability is optimisation assistance — though not all of it: it stays detectable at every noise level tested.
1c
Domain · the looped literature's own tasks

Parity: where path integration is provably the right primitive

Everything above is navigation. The looped-transformer literature benchmarks something else — tasks with a known iterative solution, evaluated for length generalization (Fan et al., arXiv 2409.15647: parity, binary addition, copying). Match-Query has no such solution, so this project's loop results sat beside that literature rather than inside it. This section puts both mechanisms on its benchmark.

A prediction written before the run. MapFormer's angle is θ = ω·cumsum(Δ(xt)) wrapped mod 2π. Parity is a running sum mod 2. A model that learns Δ = π for “1” and 0 for “0” has θ alternating between 0 and π — the answer lives in the rotation. If path integration ever helps on a standard algorithmic task, parity is the one.

Giving the coarse level a clean re-index, rather than a pooled position, is what unlocks the gain.

Where it helps

Compositional transfer (~+0.11 over flat, in both backbones), largest when motif reuse is high (n_templates=2: +0.195) and for weaker base models (MiniGrid: +0.096 for the weakest arm, +0.002 for the strongest). The gain is generic multi-scale compression.

Where it doesn't

Exact recall — path integration, not hierarchy, drives that (hierarchy adds ~0). And when motif reuse is low (n_templates=16), flat overtakes. Hierarchy helps only where a lossy summary is a sufficient statistic.

The two axes are orthogonal: path integration → exact positional recall; hierarchy → compositional transfer.

Parity is a tree reduction — the partial parity of a pooled pair is exactly a sufficient statistic, with zero loss — so it is the single case the principle says must win. A methodological catch worth carrying. Both L=512 effects sit at t = 2.23 and 2.44, under this project's t > 2.8 bar, and would have been filed as “unmeasured”. Twelve of twelve seeds in the same direction is p = 0.0005 by a sign test. The MDE rule is conservative for small consistent paired effects — report the sign count beside it, and never read t < 2.8 as absence when the signs are lopsided.

Reference

Experimental setup

Every task shares one data format and one training recipe; the environments differ in what the actions and observations mean.

Data format — a single interleaved stream

Each episode is one token sequence s = [a₁, o₁, a₂, o₂, …] in a unified vocabulary (actions, then observation types, then a blank). The model predicts the next token; the loss is masked to observation positions at revisited cells (the paper's self-supervised objective) or the task-specific target below. The model must learn to separate the action and observation channels itself.

Position code — path integration

MapFormer turns each action token into a small displacement Δ and computes position as θ = ω · cumsum(Δ), a rotation applied to attention (RoPE-style). Index baselines replace θ with the token's sequence position. This is the single axis the study isolates.

Method

How to read these numbers

Three conventions make the tables comparable. They apply to every result above.

Read against the floor

Each table states its own measured floor (always-predict-blank / chance), which ranges from 0.06 to 0.80 across tasks. A raw score is only meaningful relative to its column's floor.

Compare within a batch

Every cross-model comparison here is trained in one batch, seed-matched and parameter-matched. Numbers from different batches (or different tables) are not directly comparable.

Failure rates, not point estimates

Outcomes here are bimodal, not noisy-around-a-mean: the same configuration lands at 0.448 or 0.990. Retraining with identical seeds and settings does not reproduce (mean per-seed drift 0.185), so the seed does not index the basin. What matters is the failure rate, and n must be large enough to estimate a distribution — three seeds agreeing is the shape of this trap, not evidence against it.

Schedule matters more than it should. Numbers trained under LinearLR(1→0) — which decays from step one with no warmup — can be measuring time-to-solve rather than capability: on a plateau-then-cliff landscape a run can never escape late. Refitting one arm with 5% warmup + cosine moved it from 0.448 to 0.990 on the same task and inverted the sign of the headline effect. Tables marked withdrawn above are the ones this caught.

Paper reference (Table 2, 2D): MapWM 0.99 / 0.99 / 0.96, MapEM-os 1.0 / 0.99 / 0.97 (IID / OOD-d / OOD-s).
