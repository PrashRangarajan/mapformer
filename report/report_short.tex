\begin{abstract}
\noindent
MapFormer accumulates a content-dependent, signed phase and applies it as a rotation in attention. We measure
what that accumulated ``where'' buys when the observation map is redrawn at test, so that every evaluation
measures transfer to a new instance of a known structure. On the paper's task under a converged recipe, path
integration is the larger factor on raw accuracy at every length ($+0.243$ at training length, $+0.359$ at eight
times it, $n=8$). It pays under three conditions. Input must be integrable: under turn-and-forward actions the
advantage over an index code falls from $+0.438$ to $+0.050$, and recording realised displacement restores it to
$+0.488$. Increments must cancel: on the torus at eight times the training length, forcing them non-negative
costs $0.363$ raw and $0.280$ at matched loss (loss-matched verdicts depend on the pool), extending a known result
to navigation. Solutions must be findable: single-origin MapEM represents a varying-offset recall task exactly yet
trails MapWM by $0.375$ from scratch at a shared budget. Explicit correction and gating add no measurable accuracy
at training length, pooling's only detectable gain is ceiling-level (its larger gains are directional); recursion adds accuracy where the base model trains
unreliably (on Match-Query, in an unpaired analysis). Transfer across a change of structure is not tested.
\end{abstract}

%======================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================

A model that knows \emph{where} it is, separately from \emph{what} it sees there, should be able to carry that
knowledge into a place it has never observed. This is the claim of the Tolman--Eichenbaum Machine
\citep{whittington2020tem}: structural knowledge is invariant across environments, sensory content is specific to
one, memory stores their conjunction, and the factorisation is what buys transfer. This report asks when a
transformer's positional mechanism can serve as that structural code.

MapFormer \citep{rambaud2025mapformer} carries the factorisation into attention. Each token, action or
observation, contributes a learned increment to a phase; the phase is accumulated by a prefix sum and applied as
a rotation. In MapFormer-WM (MapWM) the rotation acts on content-derived queries and keys; in MapFormer-EM (MapEM)
it acts on two learned position vectors, producing a position kernel that multiplies a content score. Except for
two appendix baselines of the full report, every evaluation here redraws the observation map from a held-out
seed, so only structure can carry over: every accuracy below measures transfer to a new instance of a known
structure. None of it measures transfer across a change of structure (topology or action space), the stronger
form of the TEM claim.

\paragraph{What is already known.} The two-slot taxonomy of rotary logits is due to GRAPE
\citep{zhang2026grape} and \citet{puranik2026group}; \citet{vetcha2026infinite} states the two-slot decomposition
independently. Content-dependent rotation is published by Mamba-3 \citep{lahoti2026mamba3} and Selective RoPE
\citep{movahedi2026srope}. That non-negative state transitions cannot track parity and that negative values
restore it is due to \citet{sarrof2024expressive} and \citet{grazzi2025unlocking}; Selective RoPE's Section~4.2
carries it into rotations in attention. That a fixed index code cannot count contextually and a content-gated
position can is CoPE's claim \citep{golovneva2024cope}. MapFormer's two variants follow
\citet{whittington2025tale}. That a weight-shared block applied repeatedly can substitute for depth is the
premise of Mixture-of-Recursions \citep{bae2025mor}.

\paragraph{The argument.} On tasks where the observation map is redrawn at test, the transferable part of
MapFormer is its accumulated, content-driven position phase. That phase pays under three conditions, each
supported by an intervention on its own task; that rank acts through conditioning is descriptive. The input must
make a token's displacement fixed; the increment must be signed and well-conditioned so that it can cancel; and
training must find the solution. The conditions are not claimed to be sufficient, they are not an exhaustive
list, and no single task shows all three binding at once. The report makes five claims:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item The accumulated phase carries transfer (Section~\ref{sec:claim1}).
\item The environment must make displacement a function of the token (Section~\ref{sec:claim2}).
\item The increment must cancel, and cancel cleanly (Section~\ref{sec:claim3}).
\item A shared kernel is representable but harder to find (Section~\ref{sec:claim4}).
\item What added machinery buys: only recursion measurably adds more than a ceiling-level amount of accuracy at
training length on the tasks tested (Section~\ref{sec:claim5}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{The accumulator.} MapFormer maps every token $x_t$ (action or observation) through a rank-$r$
bottleneck to a phase increment and accumulates it:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_t = W_{\mathrm{out}} W_{\mathrm{in}}\, x_t \in \R^{\nb},\qquad
S_t = \sum_{u\le t} \Delta_u,\qquad
\theta_t = \omega \odot S_t ,
\label{eq:accum}
\end{equation}
with $\nb$ frequency blocks and learned angular velocities $\omega$. The paper uses $r=2$. Index RoPE
\citep{su2021roformer} is the special case $\Delta_t\equiv 1$. The attention between positions $t$ and $s$ depends
on $S_t-S_s$, the interval sum of the increments between them.

\paragraph{WM and EM.} MapEM's attention is $\softmax(A_X\odot A_P)$, a Hadamard product of a content score
$A_X$ and a position score built from two learned vectors,
\begin{equation}
A_P[t,s] = q_0^\top R(\theta_s-\theta_t)\, k_0 = \sum_i a_i \cos\!\big(\omega_i (S_t-S_s) + \phi_i\big) =: \kappa(\Delta S).
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
One kernel $\kappa$ serves every query--key pair. The paper uses separate $q_0$ and $k_0$ (App.~A.7 of v4); the
ablation used throughout is a single shared origin $p_0$ ($q_0=k_0=p_0$, so $\phi_i=0$). The Hadamard product is
exactly TEM's conjunction, and the sign of $\kappa$ is a gauge: $A_X\odot(-\kappa) = (-A_X)\odot\kappa$. MapWM
rotates content-derived $Q,K$: $Q_t^\top R(\theta_s-\theta_t)K_s = \sum_b |Q_b||K_b|\cos(\omega_b\,\Delta S +
\phi_b(q,k))$, so amplitudes and phases are set per block \emph{and per pair} by content. MapWM is therefore not
an additive combination of a content and a position score.

\paragraph{Tasks.} Table~\ref{tab:tasks}. Every task evaluates on held-out episodes and, where there is an
observation map, on a map drawn from a held-out seed. A task is used only after it passes shortcut gates run on the task code
itself: $n$-gram predictors of orders 1--5 on the action or answer stream alone and task-specific trivial predictors
must sit at the chance or floor rate.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Every contrast is a difference paired by seed. We call it \emph{detectable} when it
exceeds the smallest effect the design could reliably detect, and \emph{unmeasured} otherwise (which is not
evidence of zero); a negative result is \emph{powered} when the design could have detected the effect it dismisses.
The full report gives every effect with its minimum detectable effect and seed counts. Results at $n\le3$ are
exploratory. Compared arms were trained in one batch with the same code; most batches from September 2026 were
pre-registered, verdicts are reported as registered, and post-hoc analyses are labelled. Where accuracy tracks final
training loss, contrasts are also given \emph{loss-matched} (at equal final loss, by pooled regression), a control
whose verdict depends on the pool. The \emph{paper} recipe is 16 epochs with linear decay from step
one at lr $3\times10^{-4}$; the converged \emph{torus} recipe is 300 epochs, 5\% warmup and cosine, lr $10^{-3}$.
Match-Query does not reproduce across batches or same-seed retrains, so its seed-paired contrasts are unreliable.

\paragraph{The paper task.} Table~\ref{tab:paper2x2} crosses the encoding (RoPE or PoPE) with the source of the
angle (the index, or MapFormer's path integral), with $r=4$ path-integrated arms added; all three registered
predictions held. The position main effect at $r=2$ is $+0.243$ at training length and
$+0.359$ at eight times it, both detectable. The encoding main effect is $-0.049$ and $+0.189$ at the same
lengths, both detectable; at training length it is inside the registered $0.05$ bound by $0.001$, and it is
negative mostly because PoPE lowers the index code. Under the paper's recipe, in a different batch and evaluator,
index models sit just above the 0.506 blank floor and the position main effect at training length is $+0.461$ (its
detectability was not recorded), with the encoding main effect $+0.003$, unmeasured; both index arms score higher under the
converged recipe (\arm{RoPE} $0.530\to0.805$, \arm{PoPE-Flat} $0.509\to0.679$). The maps are used: destroying
the context on trained models ($n=3$, exploratory by $n$), \arm{Vanilla} falls from $0.9889$ intact to $0.1783$
with actions resampled from another episode, below the blank floor.

\paragraph{Why the position effect is not loss-matched.} Accuracy tracks final training loss closely here ($r=-0.961$ at $T=128$), which would normally call for comparing arms at matched loss, to separate a model that represents the task better from one that merely trained further. For position that comparison does not exist: index arms end training at loss $0.68$--$0.96$ and path-integrated arms at $0.00$--$0.38$, because an index code cannot fit the task. Matching on loss would remove the very difference being measured, so the position effect is reported on raw accuracy only. Whether path integration represents the map, rather than only fitting it, is shown by the interventions of Sections~\ref{sec:claim2}--\ref{sec:claim4}, not by this table. The encoding effect, whose arms do share a loss range, survives loss-matching at every length (\texttt{PAPER2X2\_RESULTS.md}).

\paragraph{Other tasks.} Match-Query withholds observations in the query phase, which closes the retrace route that
lets an index model exceed the floor on the paper task. On the $128^2$ map, with eight seeds per arm pooled from
three launches, a one-layer path-integrated model scores
$0.456\pm0.220$ and a parameter-matched index model $0.108\pm0.025$, against chance $0.0625$; the paired estimate $+0.348$ is unreliable, because same-seed retrains on this task drift by $0.185$ per seed, and no
unpaired test of this contrast was run. On $k$-back recency, path-integrated minus index is $+0.750$ at
$T=1024$, detectable; index arms score $0.99$--$1.00$ at $k=1$ and $0.11$--$0.13$ at $k=64$. That a fixed
index code cannot count contextually is CoPE's published claim; this is a replication.

\section{Claim 2: the environment must make displacement a function of the token}
\label{sec:claim2}
%======================================================================

MapFormer adds a fixed increment per token. Under turn-and-forward actions the displacement produced by
``forward'' depends on the accumulated heading, which no fixed increment can represent. On the torus the position
effect falls from $+0.438$ to $+0.050$ under rotation-based actions, and recording the realised displacement
instead restores it to $+0.488$ ($n=8$). On MiniGrid with allocentric actions, path integration beats an index
code only at $r=4$ ($+0.034$, detectable). On MiniWorld the effect does not shrink as observation
aliasing falls: in converged cells it is $+0.178$ at 32 cells per token ($n=5$, 400 epochs) and $+0.305$ at 2
($n=3$, 800 epochs, exploratory).

\paragraph{Rotation and allocentric recording.} Five properties differ between the torus and MiniGrid. Turning
each on separately in the torus code singled out rotation-based actions, which were re-run at eight seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:knob}). Under the allocentric record the agent's dynamics are byte-identical to the rotation
condition; only the recorded action token changes, from the commanded turn or forward to the realised absolute
displacement. The answer-stream $n$-gram gates are identical to three decimals under both records, and the index
arm stays at the floor under both. The recoding supplies an integrable input, not the target: the result shows
that a fixed per-token increment cannot perform the heading-to-displacement computation, not that a map can do
without it. It requires the agent's heading to be known. At 12 headings, with real-valued position, every
path-integrated seed is above every index seed at every budget tried ($n=3$ per budget); the direction is an
existence statement, the magnitude exploratory.

\paragraph{MiniGrid.} On DoorKey-16x16 with the environment's own turn-and-forward actions, index arms come first
(\arm{PoPE-Hier} $0.955\pm0.003$ at $T=1024$, with the paper's \arm{MapWM-Flat} last at $0.823\pm0.088$; $n=8$,
floor 0.490), the regime the knob sweep predicts; the main effects there have no recorded uncertainty and are directional.
With actions recorded as realised grid displacement (one batch, $n=8$), \arm{Vanilla\_r4} minus \arm{RoPE} is
$+0.034$ at $T=1024$, detectable; at the paper's $r=2$ the contrast is $-0.010$, unmeasured. The effect is small, on a benchmark where every arm is far above the floor; the claim is the sign
conditioned on $r=4$, not its size.

\paragraph{Caveats.} The knob effects have no recorded uncertainty, and the allocentric arm was trained at matched
supervised events, four times the baseline's batches. On MiniWorld, the falsification of the aliasing account
rests on two converged cells that differ in budget and $n$, and no test of their difference was run; the two arms'
final losses do not overlap at $n_{\mathrm{obs}}=64$ and $256$, so that study measures ``optimises better'', not
``represents better at equal fit''.

map extent, Habitat).

\section{Claim 3: the increment must cancel, and cancel cleanly}
\label{sec:claim3}
%======================================================================

For the interval sum to measure net displacement, opposite actions must produce cancelling increments, and the
basis they live in must be well conditioned. Forcing the increment non-negative costs MapWM $0.363$ raw and
$0.280$ at matched loss on the torus at $T=1024$, both detectable, and at matched loss leaves no measurable gain over
an index code (raw, the monotone arm stays ahead of it, $+0.213$); Selective RoPE's generator pays the same raw cost.
The paper's $r=2$ bottleneck learns a skewed basis, and $r=4$ adds $+0.085$ at $T=1024$ for 384 parameters. For the two WM-type generators the constraint's cost is large on a
map task and small on a counting task.

\paragraph{Sign.} Six arms, twelve seeds, one batch, torus recipe: a signed arm, \arm{Abs\_r4} ($|\Delta|$, the
one-operation isolation), the non-negative parameterisations of GRAPE's additive path and CARoPE
\citep{veisi2025carope}, a construction control, and index \arm{RoPE}; all five MapFormer arms have 204,757
parameters. At matched loss, signed path integration beats the index code by $+0.123$ and $+0.195$ at $T=512$ and
$1024$, both detectable, and the monotone arm beats it nowhere; on raw accuracy the monotone arm is
detectably above the index code at every length, because \arm{RoPE} trains to a much higher loss. At $T=128$ the
signed arm sits at $1.000\pm0.000$, so the pre-registered training-length accuracy discriminator could not show a
deficit, a design error recorded before any result was read; the training-length effect is in the loss, higher
for every constrained arm on every seed. The opposition score $\|D(+a)+D(-a)\|/\overline{\|D\|}$ is 0 when
opposite actions cancel and 2 when they are identical: $0.125$ and $0.106$ for the signed arm, against
$1.849$/$1.855$ for \arm{Abs} and $1.981$/$1.977$ for \arm{CARoPE} (seeds 0--2). Cancellation chooses what the
accumulator measures: signed increments give net displacement, a map; monotone increments give path length, a
clock. On recency (Table~\ref{tab:monotone}) the constraint's raw cost is small for both WM-type generators (unmeasured for
MapWM, detectable for Selective RoPE's generator), but single-origin MapEM pays $0.198$ raw; the registered verdict on that batch's MapEM question is unresolved. The sign
axis and the parity theorem are due to \citet{sarrof2024expressive} and \citet{grazzi2025unlocking}, and were
carried into content-dependent rotation in attention by \citet{movahedi2026srope}; what is new here is the
navigation regime and the one-operation isolation at identical parameter count.

\paragraph{Rank.} Only $r$ varies across five arms, eight seeds each, one batch, torus recipe. Against $r=2$, $r=4$
adds $+0.085$ at $T=1024$, detectable, and $+0.038$ at $T=512$. The gain is a step: $r=8$, 16 and 32 give
$+0.091$, $+0.079$ and $+0.095$ at $T=1024$. $r=4$ also cuts the $T=1024$ seed sd from $0.064$ to
$0.012$, at a cost of 384 parameters; $r=1$ and $r=3$ were not run. What fails at $r=2$ is the basis: opposite
actions fail to cancel by half the action scale (opposition $0.4950$ against $0.0922$ at $r=4$), and north and east
are nearly parallel ($|\cos| = 0.7833$ against $0.1754$). This is a description of the learned codes on eight
seeds; the causal route from skew to accuracy is not tested. The result is conditional: on MapPoPE, $r=4$ against
$r=2$ is $+0.019$ at $T=1024$, unmeasured, so ``use $r=4$'' is a MapWM-family recommendation.

\paragraph{The main caveat.} Loss-matched verdicts depend on the pool. The registered loss-matched form for
Selective RoPE's generator was falsified as registered: its two-arm pool had non-overlapping final losses, so the
fit absorbed the effect by construction and returned $-0.056$, unmeasured.

counting gate, the accumulator exponent, packing geometry).

\section{Claim 4: a shared kernel is representable but harder to find}
\label{sec:claim4}
%======================================================================

MapEM factorises where from what completely: one position kernel is shared by every query--key pair. On map tasks
no detectable difference from MapWM was found once its initialisation is fixed. On varying-offset recency,
single-origin MapEM trails MapWM by $0.375$, detectable. The gap is not in what the model can represent:
the solution installed and frozen scores $1.000$, and installed at training scale with a content
leak closed it is held at $1.000$. From scratch MapEM finds the solution per query token, in wrapped form.
Per-pair position origins add $+0.215$ over single-origin MapEM at $n=48$, split between pathway capacity
($+0.124$) and per-pair freedom ($+0.091$), and only the freedom changes the mechanism.

\paragraph{The gap.} \citet{whittington2025tale} predicts that episodic-memory models learn faster than
working-memory models except on N-back. On varying-$k$ recency (eight seeds per arm, one batch), MapWM reaches
training loss below 0.5 on every seed and single-origin MapEM on none. Single-origin MapEM minus MapWM is $-0.375$
at $T=1024$; the paper's separate-origin form minus MapWM is $-0.137$; both are detectable. MapWM at four times the budget was not run, so $-0.375$ is a comparison at a shared budget and says
nothing about asymptotic ability. On allocentric MiniGrid, by contrast, single-origin MapEM minus MapWM (both
$r=4$) is $+0.0035$ at $T=1024$, unmeasured (no detection threshold recorded).

\paragraph{Existence and holdability.} A signed accumulator can do $k$-back without counting upward: if symbols
carry $\Delta=(1,0)$ and the query token for offset $k$ carries $\Delta=(-(k-1),0)$, the prefix sum rewinds the count
at the query. Installed in the position pathway and frozen, with the content branch learned from random
initialisation, this scores $1.000\pm0.000$ at $T=1024$ and $T=2048$ (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). Made
trainable from an install written in small weights, the model falls back to $0.642$; written at eight times the
weight scale it holds $0.941$, and with the content leak into $\Delta$ through $W_{\mathrm{in}}$ closed it scores
$1.000\pm0.000$, meeting the registered readout on every seed (the paired accuracy contrast, $+0.059$, is at
ceiling and unmeasured).

\paragraph{Search.} Because $\theta$ enters attention through cosines and sines, a block's rewind only has to hold
modulo $2\pi/\omega_i$. Read as a function rather than a linear slope (five from-scratch MapEM arms, 24 seeds each),
93--97\% of solved large-$k$ cells are retrieved through the query token's own wrapped rewind, to the kernel's peak
when $A_X$ is positive and its trough when it is negative, and a fraction $0.000$ of failed cells carry a rewind (a
description; the route table was not registered). With one shared $k$, single-origin MapEM finds a 63-symbol rewind
on most seeds, all wrapped. Queries per token is the currency: with $k$ drawn from a set of $m$ offsets, at a fixed
budget $m=4$ minus $m=64$ is $+0.665$, detectable, while at matched queries per token $m=16$ at four times
the epochs minus $m=4$ is $+0.045$, unmeasured. Exposure was varied through the budget, so queries
per token and optimiser steps per token are not separated.

\paragraph{Per-pair origins.} \arm{EMPair\_r4} gives each token its own origin
($q^p_t=p_0+W^q_{\mathrm{out}}W^q_{\mathrm{in}}x_t$, likewise for keys) and starts as single-origin MapEM exactly;
the capacity control \arm{EMPairConst\_r4} uses the same pathway reading a learned constant, so the kernel stays
shared. At $n=48$ all three terms are detectable: total $+0.215$, pathway $+0.124$ and freedom $+0.091$; the freedom term
replicates on the 40 fresh seeds alone ($+0.089$, detectable). At $n=8$ the total was $+0.280$, so the first eight
seeds overstated it, and \arm{EMPair} minus MapWM was $-0.095$, unmeasured: per-pair origins are not shown to close
the gap to MapWM. On descriptive $n=8$ readouts, the control keeps the per-token rewind route ($0.948$, against
single-origin MapEM's $0.964$) and zero phase spread; only per-pair freedom abandons the route ($0.189$) and spreads
the kernel phase across pairs ($1.448$).

\paragraph{The main caveat.} Every recency contrast in this section is a contrast of fit, with
$r(\text{loss},\text{accuracy})$ between $-0.936$ and $-0.987$; the ``search, not representation'' reading rests on
the existence and holding constructions, not on loss-matching. ``Holds'' is shown on one task, at $n=8$, with the
content columns of $W_{\mathrm{in}}$ pinned: a point in weight space that the unconstrained optimiser drifts away
from.

monotone MapEM, phase freedom in the origins).

\section{Claim 5: what added machinery buys}
\label{sec:claim5}

Of the additions tested (weight-shared recursion, an explicit token gate, Selective RoPE's generator, a forget gate,
hierarchical pooling, and explicit state correction), only recursion measurably adds more than a ceiling-level
amount of accuracy at training length on the tasks tested, and recursion does so where the base model trains
unreliably.

\paragraph{State correction.} The project's original extension was a wrapped invariant extended Kalman filter on the
path integral (\arm{Level15}). Its premise is drift in the integrated position together with observations that carry
information about the true position; neither is present on the paper's task, so stochastic transitions were added to
Match-Query's explore phase (at $p=0.10$ the recorded path drifts by $13.05$ cells from the true one, and the
$n$-gram gates hold). The pre-registered prediction that \arm{Level15} minus \arm{Vanilla} grows with drift was not
supported in either of two recipes (Table~\ref{tab:machinery}): no benefit is detectable at $p=0.10$, in arms that
are not converged, while the $p=0$ contrasts are too imprecise to resolve one, so the slope itself is unmeasured. Destroying the query actions leaves about 45\% of the $p=0.10$ score in place, so both
filter contrasts are differences of two numbers near a non-positional floor of about $0.15$. On the clean torus the
filter is detectable only beyond training length, no named component is load-bearing, and there is no evidence that
it works by inference from observations.

\paragraph{Gate and hierarchy.} \arm{Gated\_r4} multiplies MapFormer's signed increment by a per-token sigmoid
(CoPE's selection applied to MapFormer's direction, 258 parameters). It separates, passing $0.987\pm0.003$ on actions
and $0.244\pm0.043$ on observations, a ratio of $4.16$, and it does not help: on the torus at $T=512$ the contrast is
a powered negative against any gain the size of rank's $+0.038$ there. This suggests that MapFormer's bottleneck
already separates the two token types. On a motif task built so that a pooled summary is a sufficient statistic, a
parameter-identical hourglass minus its flat scaffold is $+0.136$ at $T=256$ and $+0.151$ at $T=512$: directional at
$n=8$, unmeasured; on the same task the recipe change alone is $+0.160$, detectable. Hierarchy's only detectable gain is the ceiling-level $+0.012$ on parity, from $0.988$ to $1.000$.

\paragraph{Recursion.} On Match-Query, where seed-paired contrasts are unreliable, a four-pass loop on a
path-integrated model survives an unpaired analysis ($+0.346$, looped arm pooled over two batches, $n=16$
against $8$); the paired interaction with path integration, $+0.315$, detectable, has not been re-checked unpaired.
Unpaired, the loop against three real layers is $+0.032$: not distinguishable from them, at a third of the parameters. With $r=4$ the looped model scores $0.986\pm0.020$ in its
batch. On the torus the loop's training-length gain is $+0.052$ raw but $+0.006$ at matched loss, with
$r(\text{loss},\text{accuracy})=-0.956$ at that length. That recursion substitutes for depth is the premise of
Mixture-of-Recursions \citep{bae2025mor}.

\paragraph{The main caveat.} No loss-matched analysis exists on Match-Query, so the loop's gain there is associated
with, not separated from, more reliable training.

\section{Claim 6: on Bach beyond its training context, the two position mechanisms anti-compose}
%======================================================================

Two published mechanisms were crossed on their own tasks: MapFormer's path integration (position is a
cumulative sum of learned per-token increments) and PoPE's decoupling (content in the magnitude, position in
the phase, with a constant phase offset). Each replicates in ordering, not in level: on Dyck-2 a one-layer path-integrated model learns the
bracket stack where a one-layer index model does not, though every published MapFormer cell comes out
$0.05$--$0.11$ low; and on Bach Chorales PoPE beats RoPE by $-0.032$ NLL (5/5 seeds; paper $-0.019$). In distribution path integration adds nothing to PoPE (0/5 seeds
better). Beyond the training context the ordering inverts and the naive combination collapses.

The mechanism is not the accumulator: its growth exponent is $\alpha\approx1.00$ in both path-integrated
arms, and they respond oppositely to compressing it at evaluation ($-1.70$ nats for MapPoPE, $+1.44$ for
MapWM). It is that PoPE's kernel has non-negative amplitudes and a constant phase, so when the accumulated
argument leaves the range training calibrated, content cannot shift the peak to compensate. Two
interventions confirm it: shrinking the accumulator's excursion helps MapPoPE $5.7\times$ more than
MapWM in absolute NLL (80\% of its gap against 47\% of MapWM's), and restoring a
per-token phase --- accumulator untouched, parameter-matched inert twin flat --- removes the collapse. Across tasks the same phase is neutral to harmful where the accumulator is bounded (Dyck-2 $-0.046$
against its inert twin; torus $0.911$ against $0.963$ at $l=2048$). That pattern does NOT survive a
within-task test: forcing Dyck's accumulator into a clock leaves PoPE helping more and the phase
hurting more, both against registered predictions, so the explanation is withdrawn and the two interventions stand only as facts about this dataset.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
%======================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Transfer across a change of structure is not measured.} Every evaluation redraws the observation map
within one topology and action space. The TEM claim that motivates the work is about carrying structural knowledge
across structures; nothing here tests it, and sample efficiency is not measured either.
\item \textbf{One varying-offset task.} Claim~4 rests on $k$-back recency. MapWM at the larger budget was not run.
\item \textbf{Out-of-distribution length.} Most accuracy effects are concentrated beyond the training length (rank,
sign, the filter, the forget gate, PoPE's magnitude), and no account of that concentration exists (full report,
Section~11).
\item \textbf{Loss-matched readings depend on the pool}, and they are uninformative where arm losses do not overlap.
\item \textbf{Conditions established on different tasks.} The three conditions were shown on different tasks; no
single task shows them binding together, and they are not claimed to be sufficient.
\item \textbf{No language result.} The language experiments are underpowered or single-seed, and published work
reports negatives for content-dependent phase on language.
\item \textbf{Boundaries at $n=3$, and Match-Query reproducibility.} The MiniWorld map-extent threshold, the
12-heading budget curve and several appendix results are exploratory; Match-Query does not reproduce across batches
or same-seed retrains, so its paired interactions are not re-established.
\end{itemize}

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

On tasks that redraw the observation map, MapFormer's transferable where is its accumulated, content-driven phase.
It is the larger factor on raw accuracy on MapFormer's own task, at training length under the paper's recipe and at
all three lengths under a converged one; the rotary encoding adds a smaller amount beyond the training length. The
phase pays when the input makes each token's displacement fixed, when increments are signed and well conditioned so
that they cancel, and when training finds the solution. The first two conditions were supported by single-operation
interventions (the action record, the absolute value, the rank), though that rank acts through conditioning is
descriptive; the third by constructing the solution, holding it, and measuring where from-scratch training finds it.
A fully factorised where, MapEM's shared kernel, represents the one varying-offset task measured exactly but is found
per query token, and giving the kernel per-pair freedom changes both the accuracy and the mechanism. Filtering and
gating the where add no measurable accuracy at training length on these tasks, and pooling's only detectable gain is ceiling-level (its larger gains are
directional); recursion does, in association with more reliable optimisation (on the torus its gain vanishes at matched loss; Match-Query was
not loss-matched). What remains open is why so many mechanisms
help only beyond the training length, and whether any of this transfers across a change of structure.
