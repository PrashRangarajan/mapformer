\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Rotary attention writes every logit as a sum of products
$\mu\cos\varphi$. \textbf{A positional mechanism is a choice about one of those
two factors, and nothing else.} MapFormer's path integration, Selective RoPE and
ordinary RoPE all set the \emph{phase} $\varphi$; PoPE sets the \emph{magnitude}
$\mu$; MapPoPE is their conjunction and not a third mechanism.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The phase mechanisms are one machine.} Each maintains a state by
cumulative summation and reads it out linearly into the phase,
$s_t = s_{t-1} + \varphi(x_{t-K+1:t})$, $\theta_t = A s_t$, and they differ only in
the \emph{state rank} $m$, the nonlinearity of the increment, and its temporal
support $K$. RoPE, MapFormer and Selective RoPE are then \emph{nested}, each
inclusion removing a constraint rather than adding a mechanism, so every
comparison between them asks whether a constraint is worth keeping and none of
them is about expressivity. For MapFormer the reduction is an identity: all $64$
of its phase channels are linear readouts of a single \emph{two}-dimensional
accumulator.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Rank is the axis that binds, and it binds twice.} Confining the
phase to an $r$-dimensional subtorus forces the $N^D$ positions of a $D$-torus
apart by no more than $\sim N^{-\max(D/r,\,1)}$, so \emph{geometry} sets a hard
threshold at $r = D$ and says nothing above it; the measured cost of $r=2$ in two
dimensions is therefore not geometric but an \emph{optimisation} failure, and the
two are separable exactly this way. $\S$2.5 states both, with the correction that
the first version of the geometric argument --- an appeal to a kernel --- was
wrong and was falsified by its own gate.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The asymmetry between the two factors matters.} MapFormer adds a
term to the phase and leaves the rest of attention untouched. PoPE replaces the
magnitude \emph{and} eliminates the content-dependent intrinsic phase that
ordinary rotary attention carries, so it is a \emph{restriction} on the object
MapFormer rotates rather than a parallel choice --- a plausible source of the
sub-additivity seen on text ($\S$4).

\medskip
\noindent Formulas are transcribed from the implementation. Where it departs from
a published equation or from canonical RoPE, that is stated at the point of
departure. Empirical claims carry their seed counts and minimum detectable
effects; several are recorded here only to be marked unsupported.
\end{abstract}

\section{The shared frame}

Let $x_1,\dots,x_T \in \R^{d}$ be token embeddings, and for a single head write
queries and keys $q_t = W_Q x_t$, $k_s = W_K x_s \in \R^{\hd}$.
Group the $\hd$ coordinates into $\nb$ two-dimensional blocks and identify each
block with a complex number,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde q_t^{(c)} \;=\; q_{t,2c} + i\,q_{t,2c+1},
  \qquad
  \tilde k_s^{(c)} \;=\; k_{s,2c} + i\,k_{s,2c+1},
  \qquad c = 0,\dots,\nb-1 .
\end{equation}

A rotary scheme attaches a per-position angle $\theta_t^{(c)}$ and rotates:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rot}
  \begin{pmatrix} q'_{t,2c} \\ q'_{t,2c+1}\end{pmatrix}
  =
  \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}
    \cos\theta_t^{(c)} & -\sin\theta_t^{(c)}\\
    \sin\theta_t^{(c)} & \phantom{-}\cos\theta_t^{(c)}
  \end{pmatrix}}_{R(\theta_t^{(c)})\,\in\,SO(2)}
  \begin{pmatrix} q_{t,2c}\\ q_{t,2c+1}\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
and likewise for $k_s$. The unnormalised attention logit is then
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:score}
  a_{ts}
  \;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\,\langle q'_t,\, k'_s\rangle
  \;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_{c=0}^{\nb-1}
        \big\langle R(\theta_t^{(c)})\,q_t^{(c)},\; R(\theta_s^{(c)})\,k_s^{(c)}\big\rangle .
\end{equation}

Because $R$ is orthogonal, $R(\alpha)^{\!\top} R(\beta) = R(\beta-\alpha)$, so
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:relative}
  a_{ts}
  \;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_c
       \big\langle q_t^{(c)},\, R\!\left(\theta_s^{(c)}-\theta_t^{(c)}\right) k_s^{(c)}\big\rangle
  \;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_c
       \operatorname{Re}\!\Big[\overline{\tilde q_t^{(c)}}\;\tilde k_s^{(c)}\;
         e^{\,i(\theta_s^{(c)}-\theta_t^{(c)})}\Big].
\end{equation}
Only the \emph{difference} of angles survives: this is the relative-position
property, and it holds for \emph{any} choice of $\theta$.

Finally, write each complex factor in polar form,
$\tilde q_t^{(c)} = \mu^q_{t,c}\,e^{i\psi^q_{t,c}}$ and similarly for $k$.
Then \eqref{eq:relative} becomes
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:polar}
  \boxed{\;
  a_{ts} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_c
     \underbrace{\mu^q_{t,c}\,\mu^k_{s,c}}_{\text{magnitude}}\;
     \cos\!\big(\underbrace{\theta_s^{(c)}-\theta_t^{(c)} + \psi^k_{s,c}-\psi^q_{t,c}}_{\text{phase}}\big).}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:polar} is the frame for everything that follows. \textbf{Each
mechanism is a decision about where the magnitude comes from and where the phase
comes from.}

\section{The phase axis: a family of scans}
\label{sec:phase}

Every mechanism that sets $\theta$ in \eqref{eq:polar} does so the same way. Call a
\textbf{phase generator} the triple $(m,\varphi,K)$ producing
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gen}
  \boxed{\;
  s_t \;=\; s_{t-1} + \varphi\!\left(x_{t-K+1},\dots,x_t\right) \;\in\;\R^{m},
  \qquad
  \theta_t \;=\; A\,s_t \;\in\;\R^{H\nb}. \;}
\end{equation}
$m$ is the \textbf{state rank}, $\varphi$ the increment map, $K$ its temporal
support and $A$ a fixed linear readout. Cumulative summation is linear, so it
commutes with any linear map: the accumulation may always be pushed down to the
smallest space the increment factors through, and $m$ is that dimension. Three
named mechanisms are instances, and $\S$2.1--2.3 give them in turn.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
generator & $m$ & $\varphi$ & $K$ & $A$ \\
\midrule
RoPE           & $1$      & $\equiv 1$                               & $1$ & $\omega$ (frozen) \\
MapFormer      & $r$      & $W_{\mathrm{in}}x$                       & $1$ & $\operatorname{diag}(\omega)\,W_{\mathrm{out}}$ \\
Selective RoPE & $H\nb$   & $\sigma(W_g x)\odot(\kappa * W_\omega x)$ & $4^{\dagger}$ & $\tau I$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\smallskip
{\footnotesize $^{\dagger}$ our choice, not theirs: the paper states no
convolution width, and its own derivation implies $K=2$. See $\S$\ref{sec:group}.}
\end{center}

\noindent Because the state is accumulated and the readout is linear, all three
are $O(\log T)$ parallel scans. That property is shared and is not what
distinguishes them.

\subsection{RoPE: the fixed clock}

Standard RoPE fixes the angle to be linear in the token index,
\begin{equation}
  \theta_t^{(c)} \;=\; t\cdot\omega_c,
  \qquad
  \omega_c \;=\; \text{base}^{-2c/\hd} \;=\; \text{base}^{-c/\nb},
\end{equation}
with $\omega$ a non-learnable buffer. The magnitude and the intrinsic phase
$\psi$ are whatever $W_Q, W_K$ happen to produce; they are never constrained, and
in particular $\tilde q$ may lie anywhere in $\C$. Substituting into
\eqref{eq:relative} gives the familiar dependence on $s-t$ alone.

\subsection{MapFormer: path integration replaces the clock}

MapFormer keeps \eqref{eq:rot} and changes only what generates $\theta$.

\paragraph{Step 1 --- token to Lie-algebra increment.}
A low-rank map (rank $r=2$; paper App.~A.7, $W_\Delta = W_\Delta^{\mathrm{out}}
W_\Delta^{\mathrm{in}}$) reads \emph{every} token, action or observation:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_t \;=\; W_{\mathrm{out}}\,W_{\mathrm{in}}\,x_t \;\in\;\R^{H\times \nb},
  \qquad
  W_{\mathrm{in}}\in\R^{r\times d},\quad W_{\mathrm{out}}\in\R^{(H\nb)\times r}.
\end{equation}
Nothing tells the model which tokens are actions; it must \emph{learn}
$\Delta \approx 0$ on observations. The paper states only $r \ll d$, ``for instance
$r=2$'' (App.~A.7).

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Superseded.} An earlier note here recorded the bottleneck as load-bearing
with $r=1$ dropping the 2D task to $0.66$, and marked it unsupported. \emph{Corrected 2026-09-05:} ``no $r=1$ run exists
anywhere'' was wrong about the paper. The locally held copy is \textbf{v3}
(6 Feb 2026), which has no rank-split arms; \textbf{v4} splits MapWM into r1/r2
and reports an r1 row, so the $0.66$ figure is most likely the paper's own 2D IID
number rather than folklore. Treat it as the paper's evidence for the structural
rank argument, not as unsupported --- but the rank axis is no longer
untested: $\S$\ref{sec:rank} reports an $8$-seed sweep over $r\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}$
in which $r=2$ is the \emph{worst} setting. Read $r=2$ below as the paper's
prescription, not as a verified optimum.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Step 2 --- integrate.}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:pi}
  \boxed{\;\theta_t^{(h,c)} \;=\; \omega^{(h,c)}\sum_{u\le t}\Delta_u^{(h,c)}
  \;=\;\omega^{(h,c)}\,\cumsum(\Delta)^{(h,c)}_t . }
\end{equation}
Because $SO(2)$ is abelian, the prefix product of rotations collapses to a
\emph{cumulative sum of angles}, which is what makes this an $O(\log T)$ parallel
scan rather than a sequential recurrence:
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{u\le t} R\!\left(\omega\Delta_u\right) \;=\; R\!\Big(\omega\textstyle\sum_{u\le t}\Delta_u\Big).
\end{equation}
Commutativity is the entire computational argument, and it is also the limitation:
a turn/forward action space, where displacement depends on accumulated heading, is
\emph{not} representable by \eqref{eq:pi}.

\paragraph{Step 3 --- frequency schedule.}
The $\omega$ are learnable, initialised geometrically over $\nb$ blocks:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:omega}
  \omega_i \;=\; \omega_{\max}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta_{\max}}\right)^{\frac{i}{\nb-1}},
  \qquad i = 0,\dots,\nb-1,
  \qquad \omega_{\max}=2\pi,\quad \Delta_{\max}=\text{grid size}.
\end{equation}

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Correction to the published equation.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} differs from
the paper (v1/v3 eq.~17, v4 eq.~18) in \emph{two} places, both deliberate. First
the \textbf{sign}: the paper prints a negative exponent, giving an \emph{increasing}
schedule that contradicts its own stated boundary condition $\omega_{\min} =
\omega_{\max}/\Delta_{\max} \le \omega_i \le \omega_{\max}$. Second the
\textbf{denominator}: the paper writes $n_b$ with $1 \le i \le n_b$, so with the sign defect repaired the endpoint
$\omega_{\max}$ is never attained (at $i=1$) while $\omega_{\min}$ is; eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} uses $n_b-1$ with
$0 \le i \le n_b-1$, which hits both endpoints exactly. The corrected form is what
satisfies the paper's own inequality and what matches RoPE's
$\theta_i = \text{base}^{-2i/d}$. Both defects survive into v4.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{RoPE is a special case --- of the functional form, not the encoding.}
If the model learns $\Delta_t \equiv 1$ for all $t$, \eqref{eq:pi} becomes
$\theta_t = (t{+}1)\,\omega$ (the cumsum is inclusive; only differences enter
\eqref{eq:relative}, so the offset is immaterial). That is RoPE's \emph{form}, with
the token index as the clock. It is \emph{not} RoPE identically, for three reasons:
$\omega$ here is a trained parameter rather than a frozen buffer; its schedule
spans $[2\pi/64,\,2\pi] = [0.098,\,6.28]$ against RoPE's $[1.3\times10^{-4},\,1]$, a ratio
running from $6.3\times$ at $c=0$ to $736\times$ at $c=\nb-1$; and $\Delta_t$ is a
rank-2 function of the embedding, so $\Delta\equiv 1$ is a hypothesis about what
was learned, not a setting. The accurate statement is that path integration is
\textbf{RoPE with a learned, content-dependent clock rate --- which may be zero or
negative --- over a learned frequency spectrum}.

\medskip
\noindent In terms of \eqref{eq:polar}: MapFormer sets the \textbf{phase} and
leaves the magnitude untouched.

\subsection{Selective RoPE: the unconstrained generator}

Selective RoPE (Movahedi et al., arXiv:2511.17388, ICLR 2026) was posted on 21
November 2025, three days before MapFormer, and neither paper cites the other.
(Dates from the arXiv identifiers; the mutual non-citation is verified in the
versions held locally, SRoPE v3 and MapFormer v4, so ``in any version'' is more
than two PDFs can establish.) Its angle is, in the present notation,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:srope}
  \theta_t^{(h,c)}
  \;=\;\tau\sum_{u\le t}
  \Big[\underbrace{\sigma\!\left(W_g x_u\right)}_{\text{gate}}\odot
       \underbrace{\left(\kappa * W_\omega x\right)_u}_{\text{smoothed increment}}\Big]^{(h,c)},
\end{equation}
with $\kappa$ a causal depthwise kernel and $\tau$ a learned temperature. (We take
the width to be $4$; the paper states none, and $\S$\ref{sec:group} shows why that
matters more than it looks.) It is \eqref{eq:gen} at $m = H\nb$: \textbf{$64$ channels integrating
$64$ independent signals}, against MapFormer's $64$ readouts of one
two-dimensional accumulator. Their \emph{baseline} has no rank bottleneck, so no
cross-channel constraint, and $K>1$.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Their stability fixes converge on ours.} The paper reports that Selective
RoPE trains unstably at higher learning rates and that this is remedied ``by either
placing a weight norm on the $\omega$ projection or using a low-rank MLP with rank
$8$ or $16$''. A documented variant of Selective RoPE is therefore \emph{low-rank},
at a rank in the same range this project measures as sufficient. Calling it ``the
unconstrained generator'' is accurate for the arm implemented here and for their
headline design, and overstates the distance between the two papers.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Scope of every Selective-RoPE number below.} The arms here swap the
\emph{generator} and keep MapFormer's \emph{placement} --- the angle is computed
once from the token embeddings before the blocks, not per head and per layer from
that layer's queries. At one layer the distinction is nearly vacuous, since
$q = W_Q\,\mathrm{LN}(x)$ is itself a learned linear map of the token. At depth it
is not, and $\S$\ref{sec:place} records it as the one axis on which the two designs
remain uncompared. \emph{Correction:} an earlier version of this box also called the
token-versus-query source a deviation. It is not --- their own ablation moves to the
input: ``we find that this can be remedied by using the $\ell_2$-normalised input
$x_t$, instead of the queries $q_t$, to parametrise the rotation angles $\omega$''.
Only the per-layer half of the placement gap is real.
\textbf{A negative result here does not refute Selective RoPE}; it is
evidence about the generator's components in this setting, on tasks their paper
does not run.
\end{quote}

\subsection{The inclusion}
\label{sec:incl}

The three generators are nested, and each inclusion \emph{removes a constraint}
rather than adding a mechanism:
\[
  \underbrace{\text{RoPE}}_{m=1,\;\varphi\equiv\mathrm{const}}
  \;\subset\;
  \underbrace{\text{MapFormer}}_{m=r,\;\varphi\text{ linear},\;K=1}
  \;\subset\;
  \underbrace{\text{Selective RoPE}}_{m=H\nb,\;\varphi\text{ gated},\;K>1}.
\]
The right-hand inclusion is \textbf{exact}, and it is worth constructing rather
than asserting. Given MapFormer's $(W_{\mathrm{in}}, W_{\mathrm{out}}, \omega)$,
set Selective RoPE's parameters as follows.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item[\textbf{1}] $\kappa :=$ the delta at the \emph{last} tap, $(0,0,0,1)$. The
  convolution is left-padded by $K-1$, so $\text{out}_t = \sum_j w_j\,u_{t+j-3}$
  and this weight gives $\text{out}_t = u_t$: \textbf{$K$ collapses $4\to1$}.
\item[\textbf{2}] $W_g := 0$. The gate is then the \emph{constant}
  $\sigma(b_g)\in(0,1)^{H\nb}$ for \emph{any} bias, and a constant is absorbed at
  step 3.
\item[\textbf{3}] $W_\omega := \operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{vec}\omega)\,
  W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}} \,/\, \big(\tau\,\sigma(b_g)\big)$, row-wise.
  Legal because the full-rank parameterisation does not \emph{constrain} rank, and
  weight normalisation represents any matrix (rows $g_i v_i/\lVert v_i\rVert$,
  with $g_i=0$ for a zero row).
\item[\textbf{4}] $b_\omega := 0$, or MapFormer acquires an index-RoPE component.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Verified against both implementations
(\texttt{verify\_inclusion.py}): $\max|\Delta\cos| = 1.5\times10^{-5}$ over a
length-64 stream.

\paragraph{The reduction, line by line.} Substituting the four settings into
\eqref{eq:srope} and using that $\cumsum$ is linear:
\begin{align}
  \theta_t
  &= \tau\sum_{u\le t}\Big[\sigma(W_g x_u)\odot(\kappa * W_\omega x)_u\Big]
  && \text{Selective RoPE, \eqref{eq:srope}} \notag\\
  &= \tau\sum_{u\le t}\Big[\sigma(b_g)\odot(\kappa * W_\omega x)_u\Big]
  && \text{step 2: }W_g=0 \notag\\
  &= \tau\,\sigma(b_g)\odot\sum_{u\le t} W_\omega x_u
  && \text{step 1: }\kappa=\delta\text{; constants leave the sum} \notag\\
  &= \tau\,\sigma(b_g)\odot
     \frac{\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{vec}\omega)W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}}
          {\tau\,\sigma(b_g)}\sum_{u\le t}x_u
  && \text{step 3} \notag\\
  &= \omega\odot W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}\sum_{u\le t}x_u
  \;=\;\omega\odot\cumsum(\Delta)_t
  && \text{which is \eqref{eq:pi}.}
\end{align}
Each line is an identity, not an approximation, which is what makes the inclusion
exact rather than asymptotic.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Correction.} An earlier version of this paragraph called the gate step a
\emph{saturating limit}. It is not a limit: $W_g = 0$ makes the gate exactly
constant for any bias, and the constant is absorbed. The inclusion is exact, not
asymptotic.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{The inclusion is on the function, not the state.} Selective RoPE
realises MapFormer's $\theta$ with a $64$-dimensional state confined to an
$r$-dimensional subspace: the constructed $W_\omega$ has rank $2$, measured. This
forces the definition in $\S$\ref{sec:rank} to be taken on the \textbf{composite}
token-to-phase map $A\!\cdot\!B$ where $\varphi(x) = Bx$, not on $A$ alone ---
otherwise this very construction would read $\rho = H\nb = 64$ while computing a
rank-$2$ function.

\paragraph{The chain is not uniformly exact.} RoPE sits inside Selective RoPE
\emph{unconditionally}: $W_\omega := 0$ with bias $b$ gives $\theta_t = \tau(t{+}1)b$,
an exact index clock (constant to $7\times10^{-9}$). It sits inside MapFormer at a
\textbf{price}, because $\Delta$ has \textbf{no bias term}: $\Delta_t \equiv
\text{const}$ requires every embedding row to share its projection onto
$W_{\mathrm{in}}$. \emph{Corrected on audit:} that is a setting one can choose, not
a hypothesis about training. Take $W_{\mathrm{in}} = [I_r\;0]$ and pin the first
$r$ embedding coordinates to a common value; $\Delta$ is then constant across
tokens to exactly $0$ and $\theta_t = (t{+}1)\,\omega\odot\Delta$ reproduces to
$3.5\times10^{-6}$. The cost is $r$ of the $d$ embedding dimensions --- at $V=21$,
$\texttt{token\_emb}$ remains full rank, so on this task it costs nothing. Inside
Selective RoPE the same special case is a setting of the generator alone. The three further qualifications
are in $\S$3.

\medskip
\noindent Consequently \textbf{MapFormer's generator is a constrained submodel of
Selective RoPE's}, and every comparison between the two is a question about
whether a constraint is worth keeping. It is never a question about expressivity,
and no experiment here can be read as one.

\subsection{Coherence rank, and the two thresholds}
\label{sec:rank}

Define the \textbf{coherence rank} as the rank of the \emph{composite}
token-to-phase map, $\rho = \operatorname{rank}(A\!\cdot\!B)$ where
$\varphi(x) = Bx$: the dimension of the \emph{affine} span of $\{\theta_t\}$, i.e.\
$\operatorname{rank}[\,A\!\cdot\!B \mid A\varphi(0)\,]$. The affine part is not
decoration --- RoPE's $\varphi\equiv1$ is constant, so $B=0$ and the linear rank
alone would read $0$ for the one case the next sentence asserts. It is $1$ for
RoPE, $r$ for MapFormer, and
generically $H\nb$ for Selective RoPE. Taking the rank on $A$ alone would be
wrong --- $\S$2.4 constructs a Selective RoPE with $A = \tau I$ that computes a
rank-$2$ function.

For MapFormer this is an identity rather than a reading. Because
$\Delta_t = W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}x_t$ and $\cumsum$ is linear,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:factor}
  \theta_t
  \;=\;\omega\odot\cumsum\!\left(W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}x\right)_t
  \;=\;\omega\odot W_{\mathrm{out}}
       \underbrace{\sum_{u\le t} W_{\mathrm{in}}x_u}_{\textstyle s_t\,\in\,\R^{r}},
\end{equation}
checked against the implementation at $1.5\times10^{-5}$ in float32 (\texttt{verify\_theory.py}). \textbf{All $H\nb = 64$
phase channels are linear readouts of a single two-dimensional accumulator.}

Rank binds twice, for two unrelated reasons, and separating them is the point of
this section.

\subsubsection*{(i) Geometry: a packing bound, with a correction}

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{The first version of this argument was wrong, and its own gate falsified
it.} It claimed that on a $D$-dimensional lattice the induced map $\Z^{D}\to\R^{r}$
has a kernel of dimension $D-r$, so that $r<D$ makes distinct positions
\emph{identical} in phase. That is false. The kernel of a generic linear map is a
$(D-r)$-dimensional subspace of $\R^{D}$ containing no non-zero lattice point
almost surely, so a generic $W$ is injective on any finite set of positions and
exact collisions never occur. The validator written to measure the collision rate
returned $0.0\%$ at every $(D,r)$, which is what a correct gate on a wrong claim
looks like. What actually binds is not identity but \emph{separation}, below.
\end{quote}

\noindent The phase enters attention only through $\cos(\theta_s-\theta_t+\psi)$,
which is $2\pi$-periodic in each block, so the code lives on the torus
$\mathbb{T}^{H\nb}$ --- and by \eqref{eq:gen} it is confined to an
\emph{$\rho$-dimensional subtorus} of it. Packing the $N^{D}$ positions of a
$D$-torus into an $\rho$-dimensional one forces them close together: with the
cloud normalised to unit scale, the minimum separation between distinct positions
obeys
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:packing}
  \boxed{\;\min_{p\neq p'}\left\lVert s(p)-s(p')\right\rVert
   \;\lesssim\; N^{-\max\left(D/\rho,\;1\right)}. \;}
\end{equation}
Two regimes follow, and the exponent is the whole content of the bound.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item $\rho \ge D$: the accumulator can embed the position lattice faithfully, and
  the separation is set by the lattice's own minimal distance against a spread of
  order $N$. The exponent \textbf{saturates at $1$}; more rank buys nothing
  geometric.
\item $\rho < D$: the image is a projected lattice, dense in the subtorus, and the
  exponent is $D/\rho > 1$ --- strictly worse, and worse the smaller $\rho$ is.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Measured by projecting the full position set through random rank-$\rho$
maps and fitting $\log(\text{separation})$ against $\log N$:

\toprule

\midrule

2 & 4 & 0.50 & $-1.00$ \emph{(saturated)} & $-1.04$ \\

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Two corrections, both found on audit.} \emph{(a) These exponents are
ladder-dominated.} Each is fitted over four map sizes, and the fitted value moves
with which four: the repo's own gate (\texttt{ND\_GATES.md}), run on its capped
ladder, reports $-1.04$ where this table reports $-1.51$ for the same
$D{=}3,\rho{=}2$ cell (and $-1.40$ against $-2.17$ at $D{=}4,\rho{=}2$). Re-measured

\emph{(b) The note elsewhere reads the bound through its CONSTANT, not its

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{TESTED, AND IT DOES NOT BIND} ($\S$\ref{sec:predict}). The bound is

Read what follows as a true statement about the geometry of the code that is not
the reason the code fails.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{What this does \emph{not} explain.} At $D=2$ both $\rho=2$ and $\rho=4$
sit at the floor exponent, so the bound predicts \emph{no} advantage for $r=4$ on
a two-dimensional grid. One is measured, at $+0.085$ and $8/8$ seeds. The
geometric argument is therefore silent about the effect this project actually
observed, which is the reason for (ii) --- and a useful discipline, since a theory
that explained the $2$D gap as well would have been explaining it twice.
\end{quote}

\subsubsection*{(ii) Optimisation: $\rho = D$ is expressible but not reachable}

In $2$D, $r=2$ is exactly sufficient by (i) and is what the paper prescribes ($r$
is the dimensionality of the action space, App.~A.7). It nonetheless costs $0.085$
accuracy at $T=1024$ against $r=4$ ($8/8$ seeds, $t=3.57$), and the code it learns
is skewed: opposite actions fail to cancel by half the action scale ($0.495$) and
the two axes are close to parallel ($|\cos| = 0.783$), against $0.092$ and $0.175$
at $r=4$. Yet the $r=4$ latents still place $100.0\%$ of their spectral energy in a
$2$-plane, and $r=8,16,32$ are flat within noise of $r=4$.

\textbf{The extra rank carries no signal; it is slack that lets gradient descent
reach an exact group homomorphism it otherwise misses.} The representation is
two-dimensional at convergence either way. The paper's structural argument for
$r=2$ is right about what is expressible and wrong about what is reachable, and
the distinction is not rhetorical --- it is the difference between a bound that
saturates at $r=D$ and a measurement that does not.

\subsubsection*{Why coherence is cheap to keep}

Nothing above says a \emph{large} $\rho$ is harmful in itself, and the direct
comparison bears that out: the unconstrained generator's $\rho = 64$ buys no more
on the torus than $r=4$ does ($\S$\ref{sec:measured}), at $20\times$ the parameters. The
constraint is worth keeping because it is nearly free and because it is what makes
the phase a multi-scale code for one quantity rather than $64$ unrelated clocks
--- not because incoherence has been shown to cost. On parity, where the latent is
a single bit and $\rho^{*}=1$, the unconstrained generator does measurably worse;
that is the only place in these experiments where incoherence has a price.

\subsection{What $K > 1$ costs: the group structure}
\label{sec:group}

With $K=1$ and $\varphi$ linear, \eqref{eq:gen} is exactly the action of the free
commutative monoid on tokens: the phase depends on the \emph{multiset} of tokens
traversed and on nothing else, so
$\theta(a\ \text{then}\ b) = \theta(b\ \text{then}\ a) = \theta(a)+\theta(b)$, and
$\prod_{u\le t}R(\omega\Delta_u) = R(\omega\sum_{u\le t}\Delta_u)$. Commutativity
is MapFormer's entire computational argument, and it is also its stated limitation:
a turn/forward action space, where displacement depends on accumulated heading, is
not representable --- measured on the torus with rotation actions, as $-0.388$ of a $-0.438$ swing
attributable to rotation alone.

A convolution with $K>1$ makes the increment of a token depend on its neighbours,
so the phase ceases to be a function of the multiset and the map is no longer a
homomorphism. The word ``commutativity'' does not appear once in Selective RoPE.
The measured cost of giving it up is $-0.020$ on parity and $-0.064$ on the torus,
for $193$ parameters --- the only knob in $\S$\ref{sec:measured} that is free and negative on both
tasks.\footnote{\textbf{Read every per-knob number in this document against the
confound recorded in $\S$\ref{sec:measured}.} The \texttt{SelectiveAngle} arms are
not single-knob: none carries the $\omega$ buffer, so each also swaps the readout
$\operatorname{diag}(\omega)W_{\mathrm{out}}\to\tau W_2$. For the conv and gate rows
this preserves the function class exactly and is an \emph{initialisation} difference
(geometric $\omega$ against a default draw) plus one scalar; for the two full-rank
rows the class genuinely differs, since they also add \texttt{weight\_norm} and a
bias. So $-0.020$ and $-0.064$ are attributable up to initialisation, the $\rho=64$
comparisons are not, and the arm that would settle both --- geometric $\omega$
retained, one knob added --- has not been run.} Its \emph{torus} sign was pre-registered; its parity sign was not --- the
pre-registration was, in \texttt{SELECTIVE\_ROPE.md}'s own words, ``half right
\dots\ it hurts on parity too, which the mechanism story did not predict''.

\paragraph{The $K$ axis is sharper than ``blurring'', and their own derivation says
so.} Selective RoPE motivates its convolution as a \emph{shift}: ``the shift over
the queries $q$ can be expressed by a 1d short-convolution'', from an angle
$\phi_j = \langle \omega_j,\, q_t - q_{t-1}\rangle$. That is a first difference,
$K=2$ with weights $(-1,+1)$ --- and a first difference inside a cumulative sum
\emph{telescopes}:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{u\le t}\big(z_u - z_{u-1}\big) \;=\; z_t - z_0 .
\end{equation}
With the exact difference kernel the angle is a function of the \emph{current}
token alone and no accumulation is left. So $K$ does not interpolate between sharp
and blurred increments; it interpolates between \textbf{accumulation} ($K=1$,
identity kernel) and \textbf{no accumulation} (the differencer), and the kernel's
DC gain says where a trained model sits.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Measured, and the answer is neither} (\texttt{CONV\_KERNEL\_PROBE.md}).
Projecting the learned unit-normalised kernels onto the identity and onto the
differencer gives $0.423$--$0.470$ and $0.345$--$0.463$ across both tasks, against
$0.424$ for a kernel drawn uniformly at random. The convolution does not learn to
get out of the way, and does not learn the difference its own derivation motivates
--- it stays an arbitrary smear of the increment applied before accumulation, which
is a sufficient account of the cost. \textbf{The width $K=4$ used here is an
assumption}, following the Mamba/GLA convention; the paper states no width in its
pseudocode, its two ablation tables or its hyperparameter appendices, and $K=2$
with a fixed difference kernel --- the setting its derivation specifies --- has not
been run.
\end{quote}

\section{The magnitude axis: PoPE}
\label{sec:pope}

PoPE (Polar Coordinate Positional Embeddings) makes \eqref{eq:polar} explicit
rather than emergent.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Notation change, for this section and the next.} $\S$1 defined $\nb$ as the number of
\emph{2D blocks}, $\nb = \hd/2$. PoPE indexes one frequency per \emph{element}, so
throughout this section and the next $\nb = \hd$. Read $\vartheta_c =
\text{base}^{-c/\nb}$ below as $\text{base}^{-c/\hd}$; under $\S$1's definition it
would be wrong by a factor of two in the exponent.
\end{quote}

\noindent Per element $c$:
\begin{align}
  \mu^q_{t,c} &= \softplus\!\big(Q_{t,c}\big), &
  \mu^k_{s,c} &= \softplus\!\big(K_{s,c}\big), &&\text{(content, strictly positive)}\\
  \varphi^q_{t,c} &= t\,\vartheta_c, &
  \varphi^k_{s,c} &= s\,\vartheta_c + \delta_c, &&\text{(position, plus a bias)}
\end{align}
with $\vartheta_c = \text{base}^{-c/\nb}$ a \textbf{fixed buffer} and
$\delta_c$ a learnable per-(head, frequency) constant.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{$\delta_c$ is very nearly inert in practice.} It is clamped to $[-2\pi, 0]$
in the \emph{forward pass}, not projected onto that range, so its gradient is
identically zero outside the interval --- and it is initialised at exactly $0$,
i.e.\ \emph{on} the upper boundary. Any step that would push it positive kills its
gradient permanently. Measured on trained checkpoints (4 heads $\times$ 64
frequencies $\times$ 4 layers, $n=36$), \textbf{83--98\% (mean 93\%) of $\delta_c$ entries end at
$\ge 0$}, clamped to zero with dead gradient; per-layer means run $-0.0003$ to
$-0.018$ rad. For roughly 95\% of frequencies, \eqref{eq:pope} is effectively
$\cos((s-t)\vartheta_c)$. Treat the bias as a nominal degree of freedom, not a
working one.
\end{quote}
The logit is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:pope}
  \boxed{\;a_{ts} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_{c}
    \softplus(Q_{t,c})\;\softplus(K_{s,c})\;
    \cos\!\big((s-t)\,\vartheta_c + \delta_c\big).}
\end{equation}
Implemented via the angle-difference expansion, so no complex arithmetic is
needed:
\begin{align}
  \cos^K_{s,c} &= \cos\varphi_{s,c}\cos\delta_c - \sin\varphi_{s,c}\sin\delta_c, &
  \sin^K_{s,c} &= \sin\varphi_{s,c}\cos\delta_c + \cos\varphi_{s,c}\sin\delta_c,\\
  a_{ts} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\Big[
      (\mu^q\!\cos\varphi)_t \cdot (\mu^k\!\cos^K)_s^{\top}\notag\\
    &\qquad\quad
    + (\mu^q\!\sin\varphi)_t \cdot (\mu^k\!\sin^K)_s^{\top}\Big].
\end{align}

\paragraph{What PoPE does \emph{not} do.} Its phase is a function of position
only. The magnitude is content-dependent; the \emph{angle} is not, apart from the
constant bias $\delta_c$. This is worth stating because the name invites the
opposite reading: PoPE is \textbf{not} in the CoPE / Selective-RoPE family, which
learn content-dependent \emph{angles}. It modifies the other factor.

\medskip
\noindent In terms of \eqref{eq:polar}: PoPE sets the \textbf{magnitude} and
leaves the phase as a fixed clock.

\subsection{The magnitude axis is also a scan: forget gates}
\label{sec:decay}

Selective RoPE's own framing arrives at the two-factor decomposition from the
opposite direction, and it is worth recording because it names a cell this note
had left empty. Their stated design principle is \emph{rotation and decay}:
recall needs ``(i) rotation, to encode relative position while preserving norms,
and (ii) decay, to selectively discard past key--value associations'', summarised
as \textbf{``Real parts forget; imaginary parts encode position.''} A complex
diagonal state transition has a modulus and a phase; the modulus is a forget
gate, the phase is a rotation.

That is \eqref{eq:polar}'s two factors, derived from diagonal SSMs rather than
from the polar form of a rotary logit. But their magnitude is \emph{accumulated}
--- a product $\prod_{u}\gamma_u$ over the transition --- where PoPE's is
\emph{instantaneous}, a function of the current token only. Crossing that against
the phase axis completes the design space:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
 & \textbf{phase} & \textbf{magnitude} \\
\midrule
\textbf{accumulated} & MapFormer, Selective RoPE & forget gate / decay (GLA, Mamba, FoX) \\
\textbf{instantaneous} & $\psi$ (free in RoPE; deleted by PoPE) & PoPE's $\softplus(Q)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent \textbf{MapFormer occupies one cell and has nothing in the
accumulated-magnitude one.} It uses softmax attention, which carries no decay,
and adds no forget gate. On their reading that is a real omission rather than a
stylistic difference --- their classification of existing models runs the same
way, with DeltaNet already possessing a rotational component and gaining from an
added forget gate, and the softmax Transformer possessing rotation implicitly
along random axes so that ``only a forget gate is needed to fully satisfy the
rotation + decay recipe'', which is the Forgetting Transformer.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Which of their selectivities is MapFormer's.} \emph{Selective rotation},
exactly: same factor, same accumulation, differing only in the $(m,\varphi,K)$
settings of $\S$\ref{sec:phase}. \emph{Selective decay} has no MapFormer
counterpart at all. Whether it should is untested here --- a forget gate on a
navigation task with a bounded map is not obviously wanted, since discarding old
key--value associations is the opposite of what a cognitive map is for.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Does anything on their side resemble MapEM?} MapEM computes two
attention matrices and multiplies them elementwise, $A = \softmax(A_X \odot A_P)$.
Nothing in Selective RoPE does that, and the closest structural analogue is the
forget gate rather than anything in the rotation. Writing their eq.~(13) with a
decayed transition, the weight linking query $t$ to key $\tau$ factors as
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\Big(\textstyle\prod_{u=\tau+1}^{t}\gamma_u\Big)}_{\text{positional}}
  \;\times\;
  \underbrace{k_\tau^{\!\top} \check R_{\tau+1:t}\, q_t}_{\text{content}\,\times\,\text{position}},
\end{equation}
which has the \emph{shape} of $A_X \odot A_P$ --- a positional scalar gating a
content term, an AND rather than an OR. Three things make it a weak analogue.
The positional factor is a \emph{monotone} decay, so it can only downweight with
distance and can never peak at a particular displacement, which is the thing a
cognitive map needs. It is itself content-dependent, since $\gamma$ reads the
intervening tokens, so it is not a clean position channel. And it falls out of the
recurrence rather than being a decision to factor attention. \textbf{The
two-attention-matrix construction has no counterpart in that literature}, which
is consistent with the reading that MapEM's AND-gate is the genuinely distinct
piece of the MapFormer paper.

\paragraph{Is PoPE a variant of Selective RoPE?} No, and not narrowly. Selective
RoPE \emph{rotates} $\tilde q,\tilde k$; rotation is orthogonal, so it preserves
$\mu = |\tilde q^{(c)}|$ and merely shifts $\psi = \arg \tilde q^{(c)}$. It
therefore cannot reach PoPE, which needs three things outside that reach:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item $\mu = \softplus(Q_{t,c})$, a strictly positive nonlinearity of a linear
  functional, against $\mu = \lVert(q_{2c},q_{2c+1})\rVert$, the norm of a linear
  map --- different function classes, neither containing the other;
\item $\psi \equiv 0$. A rotation by $\theta_t = -\psi_t$ would impose it, but no
  rotation \emph{from this family} can: Selective RoPE's $\theta$ is an accumulated
  function of $x_{1..t}$ drawn from \eqref{eq:gen}, while $-\arg(W_Qx_t)$ is an
  $\operatorname{atan2}$ of two linear functionals of the \emph{current} token
  alone, which that class does not contain;
\item $\nb = \hd$ rather than $\hd/2$, which is a change of blocking, not of
  parameters.
\end{itemize}
Nor does the reverse inclusion hold: PoPE's \emph{content} factor is positive real before the positional phase ($\mu>0$ and $\psi\equiv0$), which no choice of Selective RoPE's generator imposes. (The composite $\tilde q^{(c)}_t = \softplus(Q_{t,c})e^{it\vartheta_c}$ of course goes negative whenever $\cos(t\vartheta_c)<0$; the constraint is on the factor, not the product.)
\textbf{The two are not nested in either direction.}

\paragraph{So the design space is a product, not a chain.} Inclusion holds
\emph{within} the phase column and nowhere else:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
phase generator & standard $(\mu,\psi)$ & PoPE $(\mu,\psi{=}0)$ \\
\midrule
RoPE            & RoPE            & PoPE-Flat \\
$\cap$ \; MapFormer & MapFormer   & MapPoPE \\
$\cap$ \; Selective RoPE & Selective RoPE & \emph{(unbuilt)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The vertical inclusions are the exact ones of $\S$2.4; the columns are a separate
choice, and $\S$4 is the statement that they compose because they touch disjoint
factors of \eqref{eq:polar}. The bottom-right cell is well defined and has not
been built --- and there is presently no reason to, since Selective RoPE's
generator does not beat MapFormer's on either task here ($\S$\ref{sec:measured}).

\paragraph{Writing the unbuilt cell down.} Substituting Selective RoPE's angle
into PoPE's bilinear form is immediate, since they touch disjoint coordinates:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sropepope}
  \begin{gathered}
  a_{ts} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_{c}
    \softplus(Q_{t,c})\,\softplus(K_{s,c})\,
    \cos\!\Big(\theta^{(c)}_s - \theta^{(c)}_t + \delta_c\Big),\\[2pt]
  \theta^{(c)}_t = \tau\!\!\sum_{u\le t}\!\big[\sigma(W_gx_u)\odot(\kappa*W_\omega x)_u\big]^{(c)}.
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The only thing that does not carry over for free is the \emph{width}. PoPE indexes
one frequency per element, so $c$ runs to $\hd$ rather than $\hd/2$, and the whole
generator must be rebuilt at twice the channel count. Measured on the
implementation:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
angle path & rotary width ($\nb = \hd/2$) & PoPE width ($\nb = \hd$) \\
\midrule
MapFormer generator      & $448$    & $768$ (incl.\ $\delta$) \\
Selective RoPE generator & $16{,}833$ & $\mathbf{33{,}793}$ (incl.\ $\delta$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent So \textsc{MapPoPE} spends $768$ parameters on its angle, $0.38\%$ of the
model, while \eqref{eq:sropepope} would spend $33{,}793$ --- $44\times$ more, and
$14.2\%$ of the whole model. Against a generator that does not beat MapFormer's on
either task measured here, at a rank knob that buys the same thing for $384$.

\paragraph{Why PoPE composes with an accumulated phase and is inert with an index
one.} The two things PoPE does are \emph{not independent choices}: deleting
$\psi$ is what \emph{buys} the doubled frequency count, and the constraint is
forced by geometry. A rotation acts on a $2$-plane and has one angle, so $\hd$
coordinates admit exactly $\hd/2$ rotary frequencies; there is no rotary scheme
with $\hd$ of them. PoPE escapes that ceiling only by not rotating at all --- it
builds $\mu_c e^{i\varphi_c}$ elementwise and assembles the bilinear form
directly, which costs the per-element phase and returns a per-element frequency.
Same $\hd$ real degrees of freedom of content either way, reallocated.

That makes the composition result mechanical rather than fortunate. Doubling the
frequency resolution is worth nothing when the phase is an index clock read over a
window shorter than its shortest period --- which is why PoPE alone sits on the
floor ($0.509$ against $0.506$). It is worth something when the phase encodes a
\emph{wrapped, multi-dimensional} quantity that must be decoded jointly across
frequencies, which is exactly the residue problem of $\S$\ref{sec:rank}. Hence
$0.994$ with path integration, and hence a prediction: \textbf{PoPE's gain should
scale with how much wrapping the phase actually does} --- with map size and with
sequence length --- and should vanish on a task whose phase never wraps.

\textbf{Tested, and half of it is refuted} (\texttt{POPE\_WRAPPING.md}, $n=8$,
grids $\{16,32,64,128\}$, one batch). The \emph{length} half holds $3/3$. The
\emph{octave} half does not: the loss-matched gain is $+0.077 / +0.095 / +0.079$
across grid size --- flat, not rising with $\log_2 N$. So PoPE's gain is not
buying relief from wrapping in the way this paragraph argues, and the argument
above should be read as the motivation for a measurement that came back against
it.

\medskip
\noindent One asymmetry is worth naming, because it is the reason the two axes
behave differently. Descending the phase column \textbf{removes} constraints and
gains expressivity. PoPE \textbf{adds} one: it gives up the intrinsic phase in
exchange for twice the frequencies, at an unchanged content budget of $\hd$ real
degrees of freedom. And in Selective RoPE's own taxonomy PoPE is neither of their
two selectivities --- it is not a rotation and it is not a decay on the state
transition, but a restriction on the read and write vectors themselves. It does
not fit their frame any more than it fits CoPE's.

\subsection{One equation: the complex log-polar accumulator}
\label{sec:unify}

PoPE and Selective RoPE are not nested, but they are not unrelated either --- they
constrain different coordinates of a single object, and writing that object down
subsumes every mechanism in this note.

Work in the complex domain of $\S$1 and put each token's contribution in
\emph{log}-polar form. Let $c^q,c^k,G:\R^d\to\C^{\nb}$ and set
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:unified}
  \boxed{\;
  \tilde q_t \;=\; \exp\!\Big(c^q(x_t) \;-\; \overline{S_t}\Big),
  \qquad
  \tilde k_s \;=\; \exp\!\Big(c^k(x_s) \;+\; S_s\Big),
  \qquad
  S_t \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{u\le t} G(x_u),}
\end{equation}
componentwise, with $a_{ts} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_c
\operatorname{Re}\big[\overline{\tilde q_t^{(c)}}\,\tilde k_s^{(c)}\big]$ as
before. The conjugate on $S_t$ is load-bearing and is the whole content of the
sign discussion below: it negates the accumulated \emph{magnitude} on the query
while leaving the accumulated \emph{phase} alone, and the Hermitian form's own
conjugation then turns both into differences over $t<u\le s$. Verified
numerically at $1.3\times10^{-14}$ against the closed form; writing $-S_t$ instead
takes the same error to $3.6\times10^{1}$ --- the identity does not merely degrade,
it fails, because the phase terms add rather than cancel
(\texttt{verify\_theory.py}). Writing $c = \gamma + i\pi$ and $G = \delta + i\eta$ with all four real:
\begin{equation}
  a_{ts} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_c
  \underbrace{e^{\,\gamma^q_{t,c}+\gamma^k_{s,c}
        \;+\;\sum_{t<u\le s}\delta_{u,c}}}_{\text{magnitude}}
  \;
  \cos\Big(\underbrace{\pi^k_{s,c}-\pi^q_{t,c}
        \;+\;\textstyle\sum_{t<u\le s}\eta_{u,c}}_{\text{phase}}\Big).
\end{equation}
(The query and key maps must be kept separate. An earlier draft wrote a single
$c$ for both, which forces $\tilde k_t/\tilde q_t = e^{2\operatorname{Re}S_t}$, a
positive real fixed by the accumulator --- untrue of any attention with
$W_Q\neq W_K$, and inconsistent with the four objects
$\gamma^q,\gamma^k,\pi^q,\pi^k$ the next display already uses. It also left PoPE's
key-side bias $\delta_c$ with no home in the table below.)
\textbf{$c$ is the instantaneous complex log, $G$ the accumulated one.} Real parts
are magnitude, imaginary parts are phase --- which is Selective RoPE's ``real
parts forget, imaginary parts encode position'' read off the algebra rather than
from diagonal SSMs.

\paragraph{Every mechanism is a constraint on $(c,G)$.} The parameterisation is
fully general --- $c = \log$ of whatever linear map produces $\tilde q$ --- so
this is a change of coordinates, not a new model.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
 & $\operatorname{Re}c$ & $\operatorname{Im}c$ & $\operatorname{Re}G$ & $\operatorname{Im}G$ \\
 & \emph{inst.\ magnitude} & \emph{inst.\ phase} & \emph{acc.\ magnitude} & \emph{acc.\ phase} \\
\midrule
RoPE            & free & free & $0$ & $\omega$ (const) \\
MapFormer       & free & free & $0$ & $\omega\,W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}x$ \\
Selective RoPE  & free & free & $0$ & $\tau\,\sigma(W_gx)\odot(\kappa*W_\omega x)$ \\
PoPE            & $\log\softplus(Q)$ & $\mathbf{0}$ & $0$ & $\vartheta$ (const) \\
forget gate / GLA & free & free & $\log\gamma(x)$ & $0$ \\
MapPoPE         & $\log\softplus(Q)$ & $\mathbf{0}$ & $0$ & $\omega\,W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}x$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent \textbf{This is the unification asked for.} PoPE constrains $c$;
Selective RoPE and MapFormer constrain $\operatorname{Im}G$. Disjoint coordinates,
so neither contains the other ($\S$3.1) and both can be imposed at once
($\S$4) --- the composition is not a design decision but an immediate consequence
of which entries of one table each mechanism writes to.

\paragraph{Why the two accumulators carry opposite signs, and what that buys.}
The Hermitian form $\overline{\tilde q}\,\tilde k$ \emph{subtracts} phases and
\emph{adds} log-magnitudes. So an accumulator yields a \emph{relative} quantity
only if it is signed to match:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item $\operatorname{Im}G$ accumulated with the \emph{same} sign on $q$ and $k$
  gives $\sum_{t<u\le s}\eta_u$ --- relative by construction. This is the
  relative-position property of $\S$1, and it is why a rotation gets it for free.
\item $\operatorname{Re}G$ accumulated with the same sign would give
  $\sum_{u\le t} + \sum_{u\le s}$, a joint scale carrying no information about
  separation. Signing it \emph{oppositely}, as \eqref{eq:unified} does, gives
  $\sum_{t<u\le s}\delta_u$ --- and with $\delta = \log\gamma < 0$ that is exactly
  the forget gate's $\prod_{t<u\le s}\gamma_u$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{CORRECTION -- ``there is no third slot to invent'' is FALSE as printed,
and the counterexample lives inside the commuting family.} The closure argument
silently assumes the per-channel transition is \emph{semisimple}: that the
interval acts by a scalar in $\C^\times \cong \R_{>0}\times S^1$, whose only two
coordinates are $\operatorname{Re}\log$ and $\operatorname{Im}\log$. Commuting is
weaker than diagonalisable. Over $\R$ there are exactly \emph{three} unital
commutative $2$-dimensional algebras --- $\C$, $\R\oplus\R$ (a degenerate double
copy of the magnitude slot), and the \textbf{dual numbers}
$\R[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, whose group is the unipotent shear
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.

The shear slot is real and I have checked it: it commutes and satisfies the group
law exactly; it is interval-invariant to $1.5\times10^{-16}$ (\texttt{verify\_theory.py}) under the perturbation
test above; and it contributes
\[
  q^{\!\top} M_t^{-1}M_s\,k \;=\; q_1k_1 + q_2k_2 +
  \Big(\textstyle\sum_{t<u\le s} b_u\Big)\,q_1k_2 ,
\]
a term \textbf{linear in the accumulated interval quantity} with a
content-dependent coefficient. Since $e^{\operatorname{Re}}\cos(\operatorname{Im})$
is an exponential times a sinusoid, it can never be polynomial in the accumulator,
so this is outside \eqref{eq:unified} while remaining abelian, per-token, and
parallel-scannable. The kernels it generates are \emph{polynomial} in the accumulated interval quantity --- linear here, optionally times a real exponential, i.e.\ $e^{-\gamma d}(\alpha+\beta d)$
rather than $e^{-\gamma d}\cos(\omega d)$.

It is also \emph{occupied}: Jordan-RoPE (arXiv:2605.04217) builds the defective
block directly, and GRAPE (arXiv:2512.07805) uses the same nilpotent algebra as
its ``additive'' family, recovering ALiBi and the Forgetting Transformer from it.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{The claim that survives.} \emph{For per-token increments valued in a
fixed maximal torus of $GL(d)$ --- simultaneously diagonalisable, semisimple
transitions --- the relative accumulators are exactly $\operatorname{Re}G$ and
$\operatorname{Im}G$, and both are occupied.} That is a real result and it is the
precise sense in which commutativity buys the parallel scan at the cost of the
non-abelian \emph{and} non-semisimple design space. Mechanisms that appear to add
a slot break one of four things rather than adding a scalar: the \emph{fixed
basis} (PaTH, DeltaNet, DeltaProduct, RWKV-7 --- the interval acts by a
path-ordered product, so no accumulated $S_t$ exists); \emph{per-token
factorisation} (CoPE --- position becomes query-dependent); \emph{the path
structure itself} (MesaNet, log-linear attention, Titans, and TAPE, which updates
its positional tensor across layers through attention and MLP rather than by
accumulating per-token increments); or
\emph{semisimplicity} (the shear slot above).

That is a closure result rather than an opening one, and it is the more useful
kind. It says any new relative-position mechanism in this family must be a
different $(m,\varphi,K)$ \emph{inside} one of the two existing slots, not a new
slot --- which retro-explains why RoPE, its frequency-schedule variants (YaRN,
LongRoPE, FoPE) and Selective RoPE all cluster where they do. \emph{Corrected:} an
earlier version of this sentence also listed PaTH and CoPE. Neither belongs ---
PaTH is non-abelian and provably outside (its Theorem 2.1 puts a one-layer
two-head PaTH transformer on an $NC^1$-complete problem, i.e.\ outside $TC^0$, on their stated assumption $TC^0 \neq NC^1$,
where every mechanism inside this frame sits), and CoPE is excluded two sections
later for a different reason again. And it sharpens what MapFormer is
missing: not ``some unexplored cell'', but the \emph{only other slot there is}.

\paragraph{Why the two slots are parameterised so differently.} The coordinates
are bounded in different ways, and their parameterisations follow. The phase is
\emph{periodic}, so an accumulated angle is ambiguous modulo $2\pi$ and must be
decoded jointly across scales --- hence $32$--$64$ frequency blocks, hence the
whole rank and residue argument of $\S$\ref{sec:rank}. The magnitude is
\emph{unbounded below} and merely underflows, so a single rate suffices --- hence
the forget gate is one scalar per head ($259$ parameters here against the phase
path's $448$--$33{,}793$). \textbf{Prediction: a multi-frequency forget gate buys
nothing}, while a single-frequency phase is crippled. Neither has been run, and
the asymmetry is cheap to test in both directions.

\paragraph{What the table says is missing.} MapFormer, Selective RoPE and PoPE
between them leave two entries untouched \emph{in the published rows}.
$\operatorname{Re}G$ is $0$ in every row of the table --- but this project has since
built and measured it: \texttt{model\_forget.py} adds
$\log\gamma_t = -\lambda\,\sigma(W_fx_t)$ for $259$ parameters and buys $+0.086$
loss-matched at $T=1024$, $8/8$ seeds, at $r=2$ (\texttt{FORGET\_GATE.md}). The
mechanism is \emph{not} identified: the gain is anti-correlated with $\lambda$
($r=-0.516$), $5/8$ seeds learn $\lambda<0$, and the frozen-$\lambda$ control lands
on Vanilla, so it needs a live $\lambda$ but not the decay. And
$\operatorname{Im}c$, the instantaneous phase, is only ever \emph{free} or
\emph{deleted}; nothing designs it. The second may be principled: $c$ is
instantaneous, so a constraint on it removes degrees of freedom without creating a
code, whereas a constraint on $G$ propagates over the whole sequence. That would
predict that constraints pay on the accumulated coordinates and not the
instantaneous ones --- consistent with low rank helping on $\operatorname{Im}G$
($\S$\ref{sec:rank}) and with PoPE being inert alone ($\S$4), but it has not been
tested as a claim in its own right.

\section{MapPoPE: the conjunction}

Since MapFormer determines $\varphi$ and PoPE determines $\mu$, combining them
requires no new mechanism --- only substituting one into the other. Replace
PoPE's positional phase $t\vartheta_c$ with the path-integrated angle
\eqref{eq:pi}:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:mappope}
  \boxed{\;
  a_{ts} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\hd}}\sum_{c}
    \underbrace{\softplus(Q_{t,c})\,\softplus(K_{s,c})}_{\text{PoPE: magnitude from content}}
    \;\cos\Big(
      \underbrace{\theta_s^{(c)} - \theta_t^{(c)}}_{\text{MapFormer: path-integrated phase}}
      + \;\underbrace{\delta_c}_{\text{PoPE bias}}\Big),}
\end{equation}
with $\theta^{(c)}_t = \omega^{(c)}\sum_{u\le t}\Delta^{(c)}_u$ as in
\eqref{eq:pi}. In code the class is three lines: inherit MapFormer's forward pass
(so $\cos\theta,\sin\theta$ still come from the path integrator), widen the angle
machinery, and swap the attention module.

\paragraph{The one non-trivial detail: frequency count.}
RoPE rotates \emph{pairs} of coordinates, so it needs $\nb = \hd/2$ angles. PoPE
assigns one frequency per \emph{element}, so it needs $\nb = \hd$. The combination
therefore rebuilds both position modules at the wider count:
\begin{equation}
  \nb \;:\; \hd/2 \;\longmapsto\; \hd,
  \qquad
  W_{\mathrm{out}} \in \R^{(H\hd)\times r},
  \qquad
  \omega \in \R^{H\times \hd}.
\end{equation}
In parameters this is small: at $d_{\text{model}}=128$, $H=2$, one layer, the two
models are \textbf{204,373} and \textbf{204,693}, a difference of $+320$
($+0.16\%$) --- $+64$ on $\omega$, $+128$ on $W_{\mathrm{out}}$, $+128$ for
$\delta_c$. The consequential change is not the parameter count but the
\textbf{frequency count}: \textsc{MapPoPE} carries twice as many independent
position frequencies, indexed per element rather than per pair. So the two are
neither exactly parameter-matched nor frequency-matched, and the second is what a
comparison has to contend with.

\paragraph{Why the parameter count barely moves: the content budget is conserved.}
The widening is not a capacity increase, and the arithmetic says why. Ordinary
rotary attention groups $\hd$ coordinates into $\hd/2$ complex numbers, each
carrying a magnitude \emph{and} an intrinsic phase --- $\hd$ real degrees of
freedom of content per head. PoPE assigns one frequency to each of the $\hd$
elements and keeps only a magnitude --- again $\hd$. \textbf{PoPE spends the same
content budget differently}: it buys twice the frequency resolution by giving up
the per-block phase $\psi$. That is the same trade as the ``restriction'' noted in
$\S$\ref{sec:pope}, counted rather than described, and it is the reason the two
models differ by $320$ parameters rather than by a factor.


\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{What this does and does not contaminate.} In the $2\times2$
$\{$RoPE, PoPE$\}\times\{$index, path-integrated$\}$, both PoPE cells are wide and
both RoPE cells narrow, so the width confound is \emph{constant along the encoding
factor}. The position main effect and the interaction are therefore clean; only the
encoding main effect carries it --- and that is the small one ($+0.003$ (the $n{=}8$ 2x2 main effect; $+0.011$ is the
path-integrated row alone at $n{=}3$) on the
torus against $+0.461$ for position). There is also a direct empirical check: if
the extra frequencies were doing the work, PoPE with an index phase should beat
RoPE with an index phase. It does not ($0.509$ vs $0.530$, both on the $0.506$
floor). The missing control is a \emph{narrow} PoPE at $\nb = \hd/2$ with each
angle repeated across its pair, which would isolate the parameterisation from the
width; it has not been run.
\end{quote}

\noindent The widening is a property of PoPE itself, not of the combination ---
the index-phase PoPE baseline also runs at $\nb = \hd$.

\subsection{Why the two compose, and when they do not}

\eqref{eq:mappope} predicts the observed pattern.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PoPE alone is inert where position carries the signal.} On the torus
  navigation task, PoPE with an index phase scores $0.509$ against a measured
  floor of $0.506$. A better magnitude parameterisation is worth nothing when the
  phase encodes no spatial information.
\item \textbf{PoPE plus path integration is the strongest configuration measured.}
  $0.994$ on the same task. Once the phase carries genuine position, sharpening the
  content channel pays.
\item \textbf{On text the picture does not survive its own metric.} Reading the
  \emph{final} checkpoint gives PoPE alone $-0.0053$ bpc, path integration alone
  $-0.0041$, sum $-0.0093$, both together $-0.0058$ --- apparently sub-additive.
  But the within-run checkpoint standard deviation is $0.003$--$0.007$, larger than
  every one of those quantities, which is why the source file specifies the
  \textbf{mean of the last five checkpoints} instead. On that metric the numbers
  are $-0.0041$, $-0.0006$, sum $-0.0047$, both $-0.0052$: path integration alone
  nearly vanishes and the combination is mildly \emph{super}-additive --- the
  opposite sign. Both single-axis arms are also $n=1$. \textbf{Nothing about
  additivity on text is established here in either direction}; the two readings are
  reported so the fragility is visible rather than resolved by choosing one.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The general statement: the two mechanisms are independent as
\emph{parameterisations} --- they touch disjoint factors of \eqref{eq:polar} ---
but that does not make their \emph{effects} independent. Whether they add depends
on whether the task has separate work for a phase and a magnitude to do.

\section{What leaves the frame}

Two kinds of mechanism are outside \eqref{eq:polar}, and it is worth being
explicit about which, since both are routinely grouped with the ones above.

\paragraph{CoPE, and why it is not in this family.} Contextual Position Encoding
computes a gate from a \emph{query--key pair}, $g_{ts} = \sigma(q_t\cdot k_s)$, and
takes the position of $s$ relative to $t$ to be $\sum_{s < i \le t} g_{ti}$. That
quantity depends on both indices and on the query, so it cannot be written as
$\theta_s - \theta_t$ for any per-token $\theta$: the factorisation that
\eqref{eq:relative} relies on --- and with it the relative-position property that
holds for \emph{any} $\theta$ --- does not exist. \textbf{CoPE is not a phase
generator with a different $(m,\varphi,K)$; it leaves the frame.} This is worth
stating because CoPE, Selective RoPE and MapFormer are often listed together as
``content-dependent position'', and only the last two are the same machine.

\paragraph{PoPE is not in the CoPE family either, despite the name.} Its magnitude
is content-dependent; its \emph{angle} is not, apart from the constant bias
$\delta_c$ ($\S$3). It modifies the other factor.

\paragraph{Mechanisms that act elsewhere in the network.} Weight-shared recursion,
hierarchical pooling and the InEKF correction are not choices about $\mu$ or
$\varphi$ at all: the first two change how the residual stream is processed at
fixed $\theta$, and the third post-processes $\theta$ before the rotation.
Appendix~\ref{app:other} gives all three in this notation, together with what each
was measured to be worth --- which in the InEKF's case is a withdrawal.

\section{Prior art: most of this frame is published}
\label{sec:prior}

Read after the fact, and recorded here because it changes what any of the above
can be claimed as.

\paragraph{The frame is a published classification, and it has a proof.} Alok
Puranik, \emph{Using group theory to explore the space of positional encodings for
attention} (Jane Street Blog, 22 April 2026), derives $\S$\ref{sec:unify} from
three assumptions and gets a stronger result than this note states. Take
$\tilde q_t=F(t)q_t$, $\tilde k_s=G(s)k_s$ so the logit is $q_t^\top F(t)^\top G(s)k_s$;
require \emph{linearity}, \emph{translation invariance} ($F(t)^\top G(s)$ depends only
on $s-t$), and \emph{continuity}. Normalise so that $F(t)^\top G(t)=I$ (absorbing the constant $A(0)$, which the three assumptions alone do not force, into the keys). Then $A(d):=F(0)^\top G(d)$ satisfies $A(0)=I$ and
$A(d)A(e)=A(d+e)$ --- a one-parameter matrix group --- so $A(d)=\exp(dM)$ for a
fixed generator $M$, and the classification of positional encodings \emph{is} the
classification of $M$ up to conjugacy:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item $M$ diagonalisable, real eigenvalue $\lambda$: $e^{\lambda d}$, i.e.\ exponential
  decay ($\lambda<0$), NoPE ($\lambda=0$), or a blow-up that is discarded.
\item $M$ diagonalisable, conjugate pair: real canonical form
  $e^{\lambda d}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{rot}(\omega d)$ --- damped RoPE. \textbf{This is
  \eqref{eq:unified}'s $\operatorname{Re}/\operatorname{Im}$ split, and the reason
  there are exactly two slots is that $\C$ has exactly two.}
\item $M$ defective: polynomial factors $d^r$. He reports finding no literature on
  defective generators for positional encoding and calls the interpretation unclear;
  ALiBi is then shown to be realisable this way after augmenting $q,k$.
\end{itemize}
He also states the reparameterised-time reading directly: gated models with a
data-dependent decay should be understood as ``learning how far to advance time, as
opposed to changing the rate of decay'' --- which is $S_t=\sum_u G(x_u)$ replacing
$t$, published as an aside. \textbf{Jordan-RoPE} (arXiv:2605.04217, May 2026) then
fills his defective cell: a complex eigenvalue coupled to a nilpotent \emph{in the
same block}, giving modes $d^re^{i\omega d}$, and --- because such blocks are not
orthogonal --- requiring a \emph{contragredient} query action to recover a pure
relative score. That last point is the $q$/$k$ sign asymmetry of $\S$\ref{sec:unify}
in its non-semisimple form; it is also published.

\textbf{GRAPE} (Group Representational Position Encoding;
Zhang, Chen, Liu, Qin, Yuan, Xu, Yuan, Gu, Yao; arXiv:2512.07805, ICLR 2026) is
this note's two-slot decomposition, published. Verified against the paper itself,
not a summary:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Both slots.} Its abstract: ``GRAPE brings together two families of
  mechanisms: (i) multiplicative rotations (Multiplicative GRAPE) in $SO(d)$ and
  (ii) additive logit biases (Additive GRAPE) arising from unipotent actions in
  the general linear group $GL$''. On the special cases, the sentence that says it
  is ``This perspective recovers RoPE and ALiBi as exact special cases, proves that
  FoX is an exact instance of Additive GRAPE'' --- an earlier draft spliced in the
  abstract's ``as exact special cases while preserving an exact relative law'',
  whose subjects there are ALiBi and FoX, not RoPE.
\item \textbf{The ``third slot'' of $\S$3.3 is GRAPE's additive family.} Their
  additive side \emph{is} the unipotent/nilpotent algebra --- the shear whose
  omission from \eqref{eq:unified} that section corrects. The correction is right
  and it is not new.
\item \textbf{The accumulated-magnitude slot, with a content-dependent
  increment.} They prove FoX is an exact instance: ``Let $f_t\in(0,1]$ be
  per-token forget scalars and set $\omega_t := \log f_t$. The resulting additive
  bias is $b(t,j)=\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{t}\omega_\ell$, which coincides with FoX's
  forgetting bias $D_{ij}$.'' That is exactly $\operatorname{Re}G = \log\gamma(x)$
  accumulated over the interval.
\item \textbf{And a content-dependent accumulated phase.} Path-Integral Additive
  GRAPE: the bias depends on ``a contextual phase difference $\Phi_j-\Phi_i$ when
  using cumulative phases \dots\ $\Phi_t$ is a scalar path variable, typically
  defined as a cumulative sum $\Phi_t = \sum_{\ell<t}\omega_\ell$ of per-token
  frequencies $\omega_\ell$, so that $\Phi_j - \Phi_i$ plays the role of an
  effective relative position.''
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What that leaves.} Not the taxonomy, and not the closure argument. On
what I read, GRAPE's content-dependent cumulative phase enters an \emph{additive}
linear-in-offset bias on the unipotent side, while its \emph{multiplicative}
angle is indexed by $n$ and its extensions there concern generator geometry
(learned commuting subspaces, compact non-commuting mixtures) rather than
content-dependence of the angle.

\paragraph{Boundary, now settled --- Appendices C, D, E and J read.} It holds,
and more sharply than expected.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{App.~C} confirms the magnitude slot exactly: prefix sums
  $U_{t,h}=\sum_{\ell<t}\log f_{\ell,h}$, bias $D_{ij,h}=U_{i,h}-U_{j,h}$,
  ``FoX is an exact GRAPE-A / GRAPE-AP instance''. This is $\operatorname{Re}G$
  with a content-dependent increment, published and proved.
\item \textbf{App.~D} is about the \emph{spectral} structure of the non-commuting
  multiplicative extension, and labels it in as many words ``the expressive power
  of \textbf{non-contextual} non-commuting MS-GRAPE''. The multiplicative side is
  not contextual.
\item \textbf{App.~E} composes the two as one block-diagonal one-parameter
  subgroup $G_{\mathrm{joint}}(m)=\exp(mL)$, $L = L_{so(d)} \oplus \omega\Lambda
  A_0$ --- and it is indexed by the \emph{offset} $m=j-i$. The content modulation
  $\Lambda=\lambda_q+\lambda_k$ enters only the nilpotent block.
\item \textbf{App.~J.4} shows GRAPE is aware of contextual multiplicative maps,
  but only through PaTH, which it critiques as contractive and near-singular ---
  and PaTH is non-orthogonal and non-commuting.
\end{itemize}

\noindent \textbf{So GRAPE's content-dependence lives entirely on the additive
side}, and there it carries a constraint worth noting: the phase-modulated form
requires $\omega_\ell = g(x_\ell) \ge 0$, i.e.\ a \emph{monotone} clock, whereas
MapFormer's $\Delta$ is a signed linear map and can represent a $-1$ action.

\emph{Scoped on audit.} ``Not contextual'' is App.~D's word for one sub-case (the
thin compression $L = EL_rE^\top$), not for GRAPE-M as a family. The paper claims
contextual multiplicative variants in at least four places --- Fig.~1's caption
(``also extends to contextual forms via state-dependent generators''), the related
work (``phase-modulated and dictionary-based contextual variants \dots\ cumulative
token-adaptive phases''), App.~F (``the GRAPE-M scale cleanly translates to
contextual versions where frequencies or phases depend on the token content'') and
App.~J.4 (``both non-contextual and contextual types''). What I could not find is
any \emph{formal definition} of one in the body. So the defensible claim is that
GRAPE does not formalise a contextual multiplicative angle, not that its
multiplicative side is non-contextual; the table's GRAPE-M row (A3 = index) carries
the same caveat.

\paragraph{What is left, stated precisely.} GRAPE occupies
\emph{index}-multiplicative and \emph{content}-additive. PaTH occupies
\emph{content}-multiplicative but non-abelian, at the cost of orthogonality and of
any accumulated $S_t$. \textbf{Content-dependent, signed, orthogonal --- i.e.\
contextual multiplicative and still abelian --- is the hole between them}, and it
is exactly where MapFormer, Selective RoPE and CARoPE sit. The three are not
interchangeable inside it: CARoPE's increment is monotone, and
$\S$\ref{sec:sign} measures what that costs on navigation --- at matched training
loss it gives up the whole of path integration's advantage over an index clock. That is a narrow claim
compared with ``a unified theory of positional encoding'', and it is the one the
literature actually leaves open.

\paragraph{Mamba-3 closes that hole. Verified first-hand.} arXiv:2603.15569
(Lahoti, Li, Chen, Wang, Bick, Kolter, Dao, Gu; March 2026). Its complex SSM is
$\dot h = \operatorname{Diag}\!\big(A(t) + i\theta(t)\big)h + (B(t)+i\hat
B(t))x(t)$, with \emph{both} $A$ and $\theta$ functions of $t$. Proposition~2
discretises this to a real transition $e^{\Delta_t A_t}R_t$ with
$R_t = \operatorname{Block}\{R(\Delta_t\theta_t[i])\}$ --- ``a scalar decayed
block-diagonal matrix of $2\times2$ \textbf{data-dependent rotation} matrices''.
Proposition~3, titled \emph{Complex SSM, Data-Dependent RoPE Equivalence}, shows
the rotations accumulate as a product $\prod_{i=0}^{t}R_i$ applied to $B$ and $C$
--- ``which, by the state space duality framework, correspond to the query (Q) and
key (K) components of attention''. Their own summary: ``our complex-valued update
rule is equivalent to a \textbf{data-dependent rotary embedding}''.

That is content-dependent, signed ($\theta(t)\in\R^{N/2}$, unconstrained, unlike
GRAPE-AP's $\omega \ge 0$), abelian, accumulated, applied to Q and K --- every
property of the cell named above. \textbf{The frame's remaining theoretical gap is
closed, by the Mamba authors, in March 2026.}

\paragraph{What is actually left, stated without inflation.} Not the taxonomy,
not the closure argument, not the content-dependent rotation. Two things survive,
and both are empirical rather than structural:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The rank bottleneck --- the \emph{input-side} one.} Stated too
  broadly in an earlier draft (``none of them constrains the angle map to low
  rank''), which the same document falsifies two pages earlier: Selective RoPE uses
  ``a low-rank MLP with rank 8 or 16'' on the $\omega$ projection, and GRAPE's
  abstract specifies ``a rank-2 skew generator'' and ``rank-1 (or low-rank)
  unipotent actions''. What none of them constrains is the \emph{input} side --- how
  many dimensions of token content may steer the angle --- as a designed variable
  swept against performance. Their ranks are stability and efficiency choices;
  MapFormer's $r$ is an inductive bias about the dimensionality of the action space.
  Mamba-3's $\theta(t)$ is a per-channel projection with no such bottleneck. The measured result --- $r=2$ is the worst setting, $r=4$ buys
  $+0.085$ at $8\times$ training length ($T=1024$ against a training length of $128$;
  at $4\times$ it is $+0.038$) for $384$ parameters, $8/8$ seeds, and the
  cause is a skewed basis rather than missing capacity --- is a claim about a
  constraint nobody else imposes.
\item \textbf{The navigation regime.} The papers above evaluate on language,
  recall, state tracking, and --- for LieRE and Algebraic Positional Encodings ---
  image, video and tree-transduction benchmarks. None runs grid navigation, and the one result this
  project has that survives every audit --- that path integration is necessary for
  in-context cognitive maps, against a measured floor and a context-destruction ablation ---
  lives there.

  This is the more useful of the two, because it is where a difference the language
  side cannot see becomes decisive. $\S$\ref{sec:sign} is the worked case: the
  \emph{sign} of the increment is settled prior art on parity and formal languages
  (Sarrof, Grazzi, and Selective RoPE's own $\S$4.2), and is invisible in the
  positional-encoding-for-language literature, where CARoPE, GRAPE-AP and CoPE are
  all monotone by squashing-function accident and none cites any of it. On
  navigation it costs \emph{everything}: at matched training loss a monotone
  content-dependent phase does not beat a plain index clock at any length, while a
  signed one beats it by $+0.123$ and $+0.195$ at $4\times$ and $8\times$ training
  length on $12/12$ seeds. A regime that makes a published-but-unapplied constraint
  measurable is worth more than a regime that merely adds a benchmark.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Verified 2026-09-06.} Every source below was read first-hand and is
stored under \texttt{papers/} (index: \texttt{papers/INDEX.md}). Three checks
came back stronger than reported.

\emph{CARoPE} (arXiv:2507.23083, July 2025) does accumulate a content-dependent
angle, predating both Selective RoPE and MapFormer, and its increment is
\[
f(x_t)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{softplus}(x_tW)+1}\in(0,1),
\qquad \phi_i(m)=\sum_{t\le m} \big(f(x_t)_h\big)^{i} ,
\]
so it is not merely non-negative: it is bounded strictly inside $(0,1)$ and can
neither reverse nor \emph{stay}. It also differs on a second axis --- $W\in\R^{d\times h}$
emits \textbf{one scalar per head}, so its content-to-angle map is rank $1$ per
head, \emph{below} MapFormer's $r$ rather than above it. A monotone rank-one clock
cannot represent a $-1$ action at all, which is the sharpest structural separation
in the table. \textbf{Measured in $\S$\ref{sec:sign}}: on navigation, CARoPE's own
parameterisation reaches an opposition score of $1.98$ out of a possible $2.00$ ---
its east and west are all but the same vector --- and at matched training loss the
arm carrying it does not beat a plain index clock anywhere.

\emph{HGRN} (arXiv:2311.04823) Table~11 ablates exactly this and concludes, in its
own words, that ``the phase argument $\theta$ should not be data-dependent'';
\emph{HGRN2} (arXiv:2404.07904) Table~1 shows real HGRN at twice the state size
beating complex HGRN, attributing the gain to state expansion rather than to the
phase --- though note this is evidence about \emph{complex recurrence} at matched
real capacity, and HGRN's own $\theta$ is data-\emph{in}dependent by design (``we
find that parameterizing $\theta_t$ in a data-independent manner is preferable''),
so it speaks to a neighbouring axis rather than to content-dependence itself.
With our own (underpowered) enwik8 result, that is one direct negative and two
adjacent ones, all on \emph{language}. None of them evaluates navigation,
which is precisely why the surviving claim is scoped to that regime.

\emph{LieRE} (arXiv:2406.10322, ICML 2025) is the nearest published neighbour to
the rank axis and must be cited as such. It learns a skew-symmetric basis
$\{A_i\}$ and rotates by $R(p)=\exp(\sum_i p_iA_i)$ --- structurally MapFormer's
\texttt{ActionToLieAlgebra}, driven by a \emph{known position} instead of by
content. Its Fig.~6 sweeps the generator's tile size $2\times2$ through
$48\times48$ and \textbf{peaks at $8\times8$}: an interior optimum, denser is
worse. That is the same \emph{shape} of finding as our step at $r=2$, on a
different axis --- LieRE varies density on the output side (how much of
$\mathfrak{so}(d)$ the rotation may occupy), $r$ varies the bottleneck on the
input side (how many dimensions the content may steer). Neither subsumes the
other, and a reviewer will ask.

\section{The relational map}
\label{sec:map}

Eight axes separate everything in this literature. They are not independent, but
no two mechanisms below agree on all eight.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item[\textbf{A1}] \textbf{Factor} --- magnitude ($\operatorname{Re}$), phase
  ($\operatorname{Im}$), both, or neither.
\item[\textbf{A2}] \textbf{Locus} --- instantaneous ($c$, a function of the
  current token) or accumulated ($G$, summed over the interval).
\item[\textbf{A3}] \textbf{Driver} --- the position index $n$, the token content
  $x_t$, or a query--key \emph{pair}.
\item[\textbf{A4}] \textbf{Algebra} --- semisimple diagonal ($\C^\times$),
  unipotent (dual numbers, the shear), or non-abelian ($GL$).
\item[\textbf{A5}] \textbf{Sign} --- may the increment be negative?
\item[\textbf{A6}] \textbf{Rank} of the content-to-increment map.
\item[\textbf{A7}] \textbf{Host} --- softmax attention, or linear attention / SSM.
\item[\textbf{A8}] \textbf{Composition} --- parallel prefix sum, path-ordered
  product, or neither.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Reading the map.} Three things fall out that no single row shows.

\emph{(i) The frame's own cells are full.} Crossing A1 (magnitude / phase) with A3
(index / content) gives four cells and all four are occupied: RoPE, ALiBi,
Mamba-3's rotation, FoX. GRAPE holds the index diagonal and the content-additive
cell; Mamba-3 and Selective RoPE hold the content-phase cell. This is the finding
of $\S$\ref{sec:prior} in tabular form.

\emph{(ii) A5 is varied deliberately --- in a literature next door.} An earlier
draft of this paragraph read ``A5 and A6 are the axes nobody varies
deliberately''. That is false, and it is worth spelling out how badly, because the
correct version is more useful.

The \emph{observation} is Sarrof et al.\ (arXiv:2405.17394), two years earlier and
about SSM gates rather than positional encodings: ``Across all non-time-invariant
SSMs surveyed, we find that the gate is always \textbf{nonnegative} \dots\
\textbf{due to exponential or sigmoid parameterizations of the gate}.'' That is
the observation below --- monotonicity as a squashing-function side-effect, never
argued for --- and it arrives with Theorem~2: such models cannot recognise
\textsc{parity}. Grazzi et al.\ (arXiv:2411.12537, ICLR 2025) then \emph{prove}
that finite-precision LRNNs whose state-transition matrices have only positive
eigenvalues cannot solve parity, extend Mamba's and DeltaNet's range from $[0,1]$
to $[-1,1]$, and show the fix trains stably to $1.3$B. PaTH adopts it on purpose
(``we use $\beta_t\in(0,2)$ as this allows for negative eigenvalues in the
transition matrix, which has been shown to boost the state tracking
performance''), and RWKV-7 restricts the range only because of observed training
instability. \textbf{And Selective RoPE has already carried it into this exact
cell}: its $\S$4.2 cites Grazzi, argues that ``the input dependent rotations allow
it to model \emph{flips} depending on the input'', and reports that ``Transformer
with Selective RoPE is the only variant of Transformers capable of solving the
parity task with a single layer up to this sequence length.''

What survives is narrower and is a statement about \emph{reading}, not about
novelty. GRAPE-AP needs $\omega=g(x)\ge0$, CoPE's gate is a sigmoid, and CARoPE's
$1/(\softplus+1)$ lands in the open interval $(0,1)$ --- so it can reverse neither
\emph{nor stay} --- and none of those three cites Sarrof, Grazzi, or negative
eigenvalues at all ($0$ hits each). The axis is settled in the state-tracking
literature and is not being applied as a design constraint in the
positional-encoding-for-language literature. Two things follow that the corpus
does not contain: nobody tests the axis on \emph{navigation} rather than parity
and formal languages ($S_2$, $A_3$, Dyck), and no comparison isolates it --- the
existing evidence is architecture-level, where $|\Delta|$ against $\Delta$ is one
operation at identical parameter count. \textbf{Both are now done} ($\S$\ref{sec:sign}): $|\Delta|$ against $\Delta$ at
identical parameter count, $12$ seeds, on navigation. Monotone path integration
beats an index code nowhere, and the learned code shows why --- opposite actions
fail to cancel at an opposition score of $1.85$--$1.98$ out of a possible $2$,
against $0.11$--$0.13$ signed. It is a replication in a new regime rather than a
discovery, and \texttt{SIGN\_ABLATION\_PREREG.md}'s amendment says so in advance.

\emph{(ii$'$) A6 is thinner than a blank column suggests, but still open.}

Two rows
constrain something rank-like for reasons of their own: PaTH's Householder factors
are $I+\text{rank-}1$ by definition, and CARoPE emits one scalar per head --- so
its content-to-angle map is rank $1$ per head, \emph{below} MapFormer's $r$ rather
than above it. The nearest deliberate study is LieRE's tile-size sweep, which
varies density on the \emph{output} side and peaks in the interior at
$8\times8$ --- the same shape of result as our step at $r=2$, on the other side of
the map. What remains unstudied is the \emph{input-side bottleneck}: how many
dimensions of content may steer the angle. That is the column where this project
has a measurement --- $r=2$ is the worst setting, $r=4$ buys $+0.085$ at $8\times$
training length for $384$ parameters, and the cause is a skewed basis rather than
missing capacity.

\emph{(iii) A7 is nearly free.} Selective RoPE runs in both hosts and Mamba-3
crosses over by state-space duality, so ``softmax versus SSM'' separates
implementations rather than mechanisms. It is the axis that looks most important
in the literature's own framing and matters least in this one.

\sloppy\textbf{CONFOUND --- found on one audit, then \emph{downgraded} by the

\textbf{That reading is too strong, and the code says so.} The two rows it matters
for --- $K{=}1\to4$ and the gate --- are built with \texttt{RANK\,=\,2}, i.e.\
$\texttt{proj} = \operatorname{Linear}(d,2)\!\to\!\operatorname{Linear}(2,C)$, both

the construction of $\S$\ref{sec:incl}, and \texttt{verify\_inclusion.py} already

against a default \texttt{Linear} draw) plus one scalar $\tau$.

not structurally confounded, and the conv / gate effects are attributable up to

also add \texttt{weight\_norm} and a bias, so their class genuinely differs. The arm
that would settle it --- geometric $\omega$ retained, one knob added --- has still not
been run. The parameter deltas are unaffected and verify exactly.
\end{quote}

\noindent Three readings, in decreasing order of how well they are supported.

\textbf{Rank is the only axis with a measurable effect, and it saturates at $4$.}
$r=4,8,16,32$ are flat within noise ($+0.085$ to $+0.095$), so this is a step at
$r=2$, not a capacity slope. The same gain is available inside MapFormer's
parameterisation for $21\times$ fewer parameters than outside it.

\textbf{The gate is not doing what its name suggests.} It is statistically
indistinguishable from the unrelated full-rank addition of comparable size
($+0.018$ at $T{=}512$ against an \textsc{mde} of $0.026$), so the parameters and
not the mechanism are the plausible cause. The natural mechanism --- that a sigmoid
suppresses observation tokens, which MapFormer must instead learn to send to
$\Delta\approx 0$ --- was tested directly and falsified: the gate is $1.35\times$
larger on actions than observations on the torus \emph{where it helps}, and
$1.54\times$ larger on the analogous split on parity \emph{where it hurts}.

\textbf{The convolution is the one knob with a principled sign, and it is
negative on both tasks at negligible cost.} With $K>1$ the increment of a token
depends on its neighbours, so the phase ceases to be a function of the multiset of
tokens traversed and the map from action sequences to $SO(2)^{\nb}$ is no longer a
group homomorphism. Commutativity is MapFormer's entire computational argument ---
and the word does not appear once in Selective RoPE. The measured cost of giving it
up is $-0.020$ on parity and $-0.064$ on the torus for $193$ parameters.

\subsection{A prediction, and the paper's own 5D negative}
\label{sec:predict}

MapFormer v4 Table 6 reports MapWM collapsing in $5$D: $0.75/0.50/0.35$ on
IID/OOD-d/OOD-s, \emph{below} CoPE at $0.94/0.80/0.69$, while MapEM-s holds at
$1.00/1.00/0.87$. \emph{Corrected on audit, twice.} First, the paper does give an account, and it is
a \emph{neuron-budget} account, not a packing one: ``performances quickly degrade,
since the amount of \textbf{dimensions} to represent \textbf{with a fixed neuron
budget} grows exponentially'' (Table~6 caption), glossed in the body as ``the
difficulty of encoding each axis accurately in high dimension, given a fixed neural
budget''. An earlier draft printed ``positions'' for ``dimensions'' and dropped the
budget clause, which converted their explanation into the one below. Its $5$D
setting is \textbf{grid size 5 at sequence length 16}, against the reproduction
below at grid $4$ and $T=128$.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{And second, the premise of what follows is counterfactual.} $\S$D.1 states
the rank outright: ``for each model, we set the dimension of its inner rank to the
dimension of the world, i.e.: $\Delta_{\mathrm{in}} \in \R^k$, $k = 3, 5$.'' So
their $5$D run uses $r=5$, not $r=2$, and the $5$D collapse cannot be a rank
artefact of the kind hypothesised here. That also agrees with $\S$\ref{sec:rank}'s
own reading of App.~A.7 ($r$ is the dimensionality of the action space) and
contradicts the ``for instance $r=2$'' gloss, which in the source is specific to
$2$D: ``in 2D, $\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}^t$ is the 2D movement vector \dots\ where
$r=2$''. The subsection is kept because the experiment it motivated was run and
reported below, and because its result stands on its own; the motivation does not.
\end{quote}

Equation \eqref{eq:packing} supplied the candidate. Had $r=2$ been held fixed at
$D=5$, the phase
code would be confined to a $2$-torus while the task needs a $5$-torus, the separation
exponent would be $D/r = 2.5$ rather than the floor value $1$, and position becomes
progressively unresolvable as the map grows. MapEM's survival is consistent on the
same reading: its multiplicative $A_X \odot A_P$ lets the content channel
disambiguate what a degenerate position channel cannot.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{How strong this is, stated plainly.} It is weaker than the kernel claim it
replaces, in the way that a correct argument is often weaker than a wrong one.
\eqref{eq:packing} predicts \emph{degradation growing in $D/r$ and in $N$}, not an
impossibility, so it cannot say on its own whether $5$D falls to $0.35$ or merely
slips. And their $r$ in $5$D is not observable from the paper. The strongest
available claim after the experiment below is ``$r=2$ suffices to produce a $5$D
collapse of the reported shape in our reproduction'', which is a candidate
explanation for their number and not a demonstration of it.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Design.} A $D$-dimensional torus (\texttt{environment\_nd.py}): $2D$
actions $\pm e_i$, allocentric aliased observations, the interleaved stream and
revisit-masked loss of the paper's task, and --- mirrored exactly, because it sets
the revisit statistics every $2$D number here rests on --- the \emph{directed}
walk, which draws an action and repeats it $k\sim U\{1..10\}$ times. State-space
size is held near-constant so that only dimensionality moves: $D=2$ at grid $32$
($1024$ cells), $D=3$ at grid $10$ ($1000$), $D=5$ at grid $4$ ($1024$). Arms
$r \in \{2, D, D{+}2\}$, $8$ seeds, one batch, the recipe of the $2$D rank sweep.

a ``rank effect'' that was really an absence of labels. The cross-$D$ difference
in revisit rate is a real confound and is the reason the primary contrast is
\emph{within} $D$; cross-$D$ comparisons here are descriptive only.

\subsection{The difference that has not been tested}
\label{sec:place}

Everything above concerns the \emph{generator}. Selective RoPE also differs in
\emph{placement}: it derives the angle per head and per layer from that layer's own
queries, whereas MapFormer computes $\theta$ once from the token embeddings and
shares it across all layers and heads. The arms here swap the generator only, and
at one layer the distinction is nearly vacuous, since $q = W_Q\,\mathrm{LN}(x)$ is
itself a learned linear map of the token. At depth it is real, and it is the sole
axis on which the two designs remain uncompared.

It is also not the axis probed by the refinement experiment of Appendix~A: that
carried a \emph{bounded gated correction} to $\theta$ across iterations of a shared
block and the optimiser declined to use it. Recomputing $\theta$ from scratch at
each layer, from that layer's representation, is a different model and an open
question.

\appendix

\section{The other mechanisms, in the same notation}
\label{app:other}

\paragraph{Weight-shared recursion (the ``loop'').}
With $B_\vartheta$ a single transformer block and $n$ the loop count,
\begin{equation}
  h^{(0)} = \text{Emb}(x), \qquad
  h^{(j+1)} = B_\vartheta\!\left(h^{(j)};\,\cos\theta,\sin\theta\right),\quad j=0,\dots,n-1 .
\end{equation}
$\theta$ is computed \emph{once} from the token embeddings and reused, matching
both parents. Parameter count equals one layer; compute equals $n$ layers.

Carrying a \emph{bounded, gated correction} to $\theta$ across iterations ---
$\theta \leftarrow \theta_0 + g\cdot\tanh(\text{refine}(h))$ with $g$ initialised
to zero --- is a different model, and it is null. On the action-noise run the null
is drawn from ($n=9$), the learned gate settles at mean $|g| = 0.144$, max $0.320$,
with the sign \emph{consistent} in $7$ of $9$ seeds --- so the correction is capped
at $0.32$ rad against a $\theta$ spanning $\sim 2\pi T$, i.e.\ the optimiser points
the refinement somewhere and then makes it negligible.\footnote{An earlier draft
gave $|g|\approx0.083$ with \emph{inconsistent} sign. Those are the Match-Query
checkpoints --- the premise-invalid run --- read by mistake for the action-noise
ones. The conclusion is unchanged and the supporting reading is not: the sign is
largely consistent, so the argument rests on the magnitude alone.} The gate was verified
escapable (gradient $1.9\times 10^{-3}$ at zero), so the optimiser declined rather
than failed to express it. A genuine per-iteration recomputation of
$\omega\cdot\mathrm{cumsum}(\Delta(h^{(j)}))$ has \emph{not} been run.

\paragraph{Level 1.5 invariant EKF (withdrawn; recorded for completeness).}
A correction applied to $\theta$ before the rotation:
\begin{align}
  R_t &= \exp\!\big(\mathrm{clamp}(f_R(x_t),-5,5)\big) & &\text{per-token measurement noise}\\
  z_t &= \pi\tanh\!\big(f_z(x_t)\big) & &\text{measurement in }[-\pi,\pi]\\
  \nu_t &= \atantwo\!\big(\sin(z_t-\theta_t),\,\cos(z_t-\theta_t)\big) & &\text{wrapped innovation}\\
  K_t &= \Pi/(\Pi + R_t),\qquad \Pi = e^{\log\Pi} & &\text{gain; }\Pi\in\R^{H\times \nb}\text{, constant in }t\\
  d_t &= (1-K_t)\,d_{t-1} + K_t\,\nu_t, \qquad d_{-1}=0 & &\text{affine scan}\\
  \hat\theta_t &= \theta_t + d_t . & &
\end{align}
$\Pi$ is a learned $(H\times\nb)$ constant --- 64 values at the default config, one
per head and frequency block --- not a scalar; it is constant across \emph{tokens},
which is what distinguishes Level 1.5 from the full heteroscedastic filter. The
recurrence is scalar in each channel and affine, so it is one more parallel scan.

\paragraph{Initialisation, which turned out to matter.} $\log R$'s head is
initialised with weights scaled by $0.01$ and bias $0$, giving $R\approx 1$ and
hence $K \approx 0.5$ \emph{at initialisation} --- corrections at half strength
before anything is learned. On the multiplicative-attention backbone this destroyed
the gradient path and diverged 3 of 9 seeds; the fix was a bias of $3.0$, i.e.\
$K\approx 0.05$, making the filter a near-no-op at init.

\paragraph{Status.} No individual component is load-bearing: the measurement head,
the per-token gate and the learned $\Pi$ can each be removed alone at no measurable
cost ($n=5$). The whole filter does have an effect, but only at out-of-distribution
length ($+0.062$ at $T{=}512$, $+0.124$ at $T{=}1024$, \emph{loss-matched only} ---
the raw contrasts are $+0.057$ and $+0.120$, both inside their MDEs of $0.150$ and
$0.180$ at $n=5$, so the detectability comes entirely from the post-hoc regression
on final training loss), and on a task
built to supply the drift it is meant to correct the effect \emph{does not grow
with the drift} --- flat in one recipe, negative in another. The mechanism is
withdrawn as inference; what survives is stabilisation.

\paragraph{Hierarchy (hourglass).} With pooling factor $k$, a causal
\emph{shift-then-mean} pool $P_k$ and an upsample $U_k$,
\begin{equation}
  h \leftarrow B_{\mathrm{pre}}(h), \qquad
  h \leftarrow h + U_k\,B_{\mathrm{coarse}}\!\big(P_k h\big), \qquad
  h \leftarrow B_{\mathrm{post}}(h),
\end{equation}
with the coarse angle pooled alongside the content, and the sequence padded to a
multiple of $k$ then truncated after $B_{\mathrm{post}}$.

Two details the notation would otherwise hide. $P_k$ right-shifts by $k-1$ with a
zero pad \emph{before} averaging, so coarse token $j$ is the mean over original
indices $jk-k+1,\dots,jk$ --- not the plain group mean, and the first coarse token
is dragged toward zero by the pad. And $U_k$ is \texttt{repeat\_interleave}, which
is \emph{not} the adjoint of $P_k$ (that would carry a $1/k$ and the matching
shift); it is written $U_k$ rather than $P_k^{\dagger}$ for that reason.

Setting all three blocks to the \emph{same} shared module gives the weight-shared
hourglass: one block's parameters (204,373, identical to the flat 1-layer model,
against 600,917 unshared) with the coarse block's token reduction retained. Note
that every hourglass class ignores \texttt{n\_layers} and is always this 3-block
scaffold, so a flat control must be built at 3 blocks, not 1.
